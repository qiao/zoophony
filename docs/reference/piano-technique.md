# Piano technique: how a professional pianist sits and moves

This file is the target of the pose and the motion of the polar bear pianist of the app. It
describes the bench, the distance to the keys, the trunk, the arms, the wrists, the hands, the
feet, the motion, and the rest pose of a professional pianist, with a source for each fact.

**Upkeep.** This file is current. It is not a record of one date, as a research report is. A
round that changes the pose or the motion of the pianist updates this file in the same commit:
section 9 "Use in the app", and each fact that the round finds wrong. Each new fact gets its
source. The research report
[`2026-10-24-pianist-pose.md`](../research/2026-10-24-pianist-pose.md) holds the measures of the
app.

**Source marks.** Each fact names its source by a number of section 10. "(abstract)" marks a fact
from an abstract only. "(secondary)" marks a fact that another source quotes. "(derived)" marks a
value that this file calculates from the values of its sources. "(a search summary only)" marks a
fact from a search summary, because the page did not open. A fact with no mark comes from the
text of the source.

## Terms

- **Key surface.** The top of the white keys at rest.
- **Key bed.** The stop under the keys. A pressed key stops on it.
- **Fallboard.** The lid that covers the keys. When it is open, it stands behind the keys.
- **Damper pedal.** The right pedal. It lifts all dampers off the strings [14].
- **Sit bones.** The two lowest points of the pelvis. They carry the weight on the bench [9].
- **Knuckle arch ("bridge").** The main knuckles stand up across the hand and support curved
  fingers [15].

## 1. The bench and the height

- The key surface is "about 735 mm" above the floor, "substantially equal to the height of the
  keyboard in natural or acoustic pianos" [19].
- A concert bench adjusts from 406 mm to 560 mm [18].
- The elbows are "almost level with the keyboard" [1].
- "The forearm and elbow should be at the same level as the surface of the white keys when they
  are not depressed" [3].
- The length of the upper arm sets the seat height. A shorter upper arm needs a higher seat [3].
- One Taubman text puts the elbows level with the top of the white keys "or slightly higher" [4].
- Czerny put the elbows "about an inch higher than the upper surface of the keys, so that the
  forearm and hand are horizontal" (secondary) [5].
- Chang puts the elbows "slightly below the height of the hands" [8]. Huang puts the forearms in
  line "with the key beds, that is, slightly below the surface of the keys" [5].
- The elbow angle is 90° to 110° [7].
- A seat that is too low puts static arm weight on the fingers. A seat that is too high makes the
  wrists and the shoulders tense [5].

## 2. The distance to the keys

- "The elbows are clearly in front of the torso" [7]. The elbows are "slightly in front of the
  torso" [4].
- The arm does not reach forward to get to the keys [4].
- In the fist test, the pianist holds the arms straight out with loose fists. The "knuckles should
  just touch the fallboard" [2].
- The elbows must not touch the torso when the arms cross the body [5].
- The pianist sits on the front half of the bench [1][2][4][5][8]. Golandsky puts the pianist in
  the middle of the bench [3].
- The bench is at the center of the keyboard and faces the piano squarely [1].

## 3. The trunk and the head

- The back is straight. The weight is centered on the buttocks [1].
- The spine keeps its natural curves. The pelvis is in its center position [7].
- The torso is "neither held up nor crouching" and faces straight ahead [3].
- The pianist leans a little toward the keyboard from the front of the bench [5].
- In one study, a thorax flexion of more than 20° counts as a posture that is not neutral [12].
- The shoulders go back and away from the neck [5].
- The head is erect, and the chin stays up from the chest [5].
- A tight focus on the fingers makes the pianist hunch [5].

## 4. The arms

- The bench height lets the arms "hang loosely from the shoulder" [6].
- An elbow that stays too close to the torso "collapses the elbow fulcrum" [3]. A locked elbow
  against the body is one of "the worst technical mistakes" [5].
- Czerny put the elbows "four inches closer to the keyboard than the shoulders" (secondary) [5].
  With an upper arm of 0.30 m, this is about 19° from the vertical (derived).
- With a level forearm and an elbow of 90° to 110°, the upper arm is 0° to 20° forward of the
  vertical (derived) [7].
- The forearm lines up with the third, the fourth, and the fifth fingers [6].

## 5. The wrists and the hands

- "The wrist must always be at the level where the hand and forearm are connected" [3]. A low
  wrist and a high wrist both break this line [3].
- Uniarts gives a basic wrist of 15° of extension [7]. One study counts a wrist within ±5° as
  neutral [12].
- The knuckles form a bridge across the hand and support curved fingers [15].
- The fingers play on the tips. Flat fingers and curled fingers are both errors [16].
- The thumb plays on its corner and is slightly bent [16].

## 6. The feet

- The feet are flat on the floor "from heel to toe" [1].
- When the pianist pedals, the heel stays on the floor [5][13]. The ball of the foot pushes the
  damper pedal [13].
- With the pedal at rest, the foot stands at about a right angle to the shin [20]. The top of a
  damper pedal stands 46 mm to 87 mm above the floor, and the pedal goes down 21 mm to 25 mm [20].

## 7. The motion

- The fingers, the hand, and the forearm "move together at all times, in the same direction" [3].
- "The upper arm and torso follow along, in small amounts" [3]. When the hand moves to the side,
  "the elbow follows proportionally" [3].
- The pianist sits on the front half of the bench, "so you can easily lean left or right" [2].
- At the far ends of the keyboard, the trunk leans nearer to the piano (abstract) [10].
- In a fast Chopin etude, the trunk moved 37° to 51° from side to side, and 10° to 16° forward
  and back [11].
- The trunk moved smoothly. The weight shifts came at the leaps [11].
- The hand hovers over the keys before it plays [6]. Teachers train a lift and a drop of the hand
  and the forearm as one unit from the elbow [17].

## 8. The rest pose

- The pianist sits tall on the front half of the bench [2].
- Before the pianist plays, the arms hang at the sides, and the wrists find their neutral
  position [6][17].
- In the rest position before the first note, the pianist sits tall, "strong in the small of
  their back", with the head up, the shoulders down, the elbows relaxed, and "both hands resting
  on lap". Then the pianist lifts the hands to the keyboard [21].
- In a competition, a pianist waited before the first note with the hands poised over the keys,
  or with the hands in the lap [22 (a search summary only)].

The side view below shows the target pose: the elbow level with the key surface and a little in
front of the trunk, and the trunk upright over the sit bones.

```text
              head erect, eyes to the keys and the music
                 O
                 |  trunk upright, small lean forward
   shoulder ---> +
                  \   upper arm 0 to 20 deg forward
         elbow --> +=========+ <--- hand on the key surface
                    forearm level   (elbow level with the key top)
   sit bones --> #####  bench, front half
                 |    \
                 |     \  shin
                 |      \_ foot flat, right heel down, ball on the pedal
```

## 9. Use in the app

The polar bear pianist of the app (`HANDS_HOLDS.piano` of `src/pose/hands.ts`, `PIANO_SCALE` and
`PIANO_FRONT_H` of `src/models/instruments/piano.ts`) follows sections 1 to 7 with these
differences. The research reports
[`2026-10-24-pianist-pose.md`](../research/2026-10-24-pianist-pose.md),
[`2026-10-28-polar-legs.md`](../research/2026-10-28-polar-legs.md), and
[`2026-10-28-polar-bench2.md`](../research/2026-10-28-polar-bench2.md) give the measures.

- **Height.** The bear is large, and its legs keep the proportions of its mesh. It sits on a
  bench of 0.255 m with the thighs 25° down, so its hip joints stand 0.40 m and its shoulder
  joints about 1.09 m above the floor. The piano is 1.22 times the size of a real piano, with the
  key top at 0.85 m. The elbow joints stand about 6 to 8 cm above the key top, because the paw is
  thick, and the forearms slope about 5° down to the keys (section 1).
- **Trunk.** The spine leans 6° toward the keys, under the 20° of a neutral thorax (section 3),
  and the shoulders turn down 4° in the play.
- **Distance.** The keys stand 1.0 H in front of the floor point. Farther keys made the right leg
  reach for the damper pedal and pull the haunch into the bench.
- **Arms.** The elbows point out, down, and back, and stand about 27° to 30° out from the
  trunk. Elbows that hang nearer went into the wide trunk (section 4).
- **Wrists and paws.** The palm faces down, in line with the level forearm (section 5). The
  claws curl 30°, and their tips lie on the key tops at each strike, 3 to 5 cm past the front
  edge of the keys (section 5).
- **Motion.** The trunk leans to the side toward the keys of the two paws, up to 10° (section 7).
  At the far keys of the demos, the outer upper arm still stands 42° to 47° out, as the arm of
  a pianist that reaches the far end.
- **Feet.** In the play, the right heel stays on the floor, and the ball of the foot lies on the
  front of the damper pedal (section 6, `posePedalFoot()` of `src/pose/pedal.ts`). The pedal top
  stands 7 cm above the floor, so the foot turns up about 20° around the heel, and about 12° on
  the pressed pedal. The paw is 24 cm wide, so the pedal lies under its inner edge, and the paw
  stays right of the sostenuto pedal. The sustain pedal of the score (CC 64) presses the damper
  pedal, and the foot follows it. The left foot stays flat on the floor, left of the soft pedal.
  The report [`2026-10-24-piano-pedals.md`](../research/2026-10-24-piano-pedals.md) gives the
  measures.
- **Rest.** The trunk sits tall, and the paws rest in the lap (section 8). The app differs: the
  round belly of the polar bear covers its thighs up to 0.45 m in front of the hips, and the top
  of a thigh past the belly stands about 0.7 m from the shoulder joint, where the arm reaches
  0.49 m. So the paws lie on the lower belly, as low as the arms reach, 8 cm to 10 cm below
  the key top, with the palms down and the elbows bent by the sides
  (`docs/research/2026-10-25-timpani-piano-rest.md`).
