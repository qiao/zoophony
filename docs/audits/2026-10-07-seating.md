# Audit of the seating against professional practice

Date: 2026-10-07. Base commit: `d70405d`. Branch: `seating-audit`.

The user asked on 2026-10-07 (`TODO.md`, step 8): "audit the seating algorithm to make sure it's
factually correct and align with professional standards". This audit compares the seating of
design 3.7, 3.8, 4.2, and 4.5 and of the code with the practice of professional orchestras,
chamber groups, and choirs. The audit made no change to the code. The user selects the fixes.

## Findings

### F1. A second viola or cello seat gets a first desk in row 0 (S1)

**Code.** `groupOf()` gives the first desk of each viola and cello seat the group `viola` or
`cello` (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:881` to `:894`). Thus each seat puts two players in row 0.
Row 0 is a split row: the violins left of 0°, the other strings right of 0°, and each side at
most 80° (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:462` to `:469`, design 4.5 lines 1182 to 1191). The
radius of row 0 is 2 times the wider side, divided by 160°.

**Effect.** A file with two viola seats and two cello seats has 8 first-desk players right of
0° and 4 left of 0°:

| Input | Row 0 radius | Stage side `S` |
|---|---|---|
| The demos with "Auto" | 2.72 to 4.08 m | 6.71 to 11.84 m |
| String sextet test file, "Full" (2 violin, 2 viola, 2 cello seats) | 6.70 m | 14.29 m |
| A Bach-like file: 3 violin, 3 viola, 3 cello seats, a bass, a harpsichord | 10.04 m | 20.65 m |

The strings then sit 6.7 m or 10 m from the conductor, with an empty stage in front of them. The
Bach demo had the same problem with its nine string parts (20.71 m). The demo pipeline merged
the three viola parts and the three cello parts to avoid it (`demos/README.md`, lines 115 and
116).

**Practice.** A section has one first desk. The other players of the section sit at the desks
behind it (S11). Staves such as "Viola I" and "Viola II" are a divisi of one section, and the
front desks are fixed (S15).

**Fix.** Number the desks of a string section across its seats: the second viola seat continues
after the last desk of the first viola seat. The violin seats 1 and higher continue the second
violins. Give the desks rows of their own: desk 0 in row 0, desk 1 in row 1, desk 2 in row 2,
and the other desks in row 3. All four rows are split rows. Extend the wedge pass to rows 2
and 3.

```typescript
/** The rows of the strings: one row for each desk number of a string section (fix of F1). */
const STRING_ROWS: readonly (readonly RowGroup[])[] = [
  ['violin-1', 'violin-2', 'viola', 'cello'],
  ['violin-1-b', 'violin-2-b', 'viola-b', 'cello-b'],
  ['violin-1-c', 'violin-2-c', 'viola-c', 'cello-c'],
  ['violin-1-d', 'violin-2-d', 'viola-d', 'cello-d'],
];

/** The first desk of each string seat in its section, for `groupOf()` (fix of F1). */
type DeskOffsets = ReadonlyMap<number, number>;
```

```text
 sextet "Full" now (row 0 at 6.70 m)          with desk rows (row 0 at 3.37 m)

 row 1:  v1 v1  v2 v2 | va va  va va  vc  vc   row 3:               | va        vc
 row 0:  v1 v1  v2 v2 | va va  va va  vc vc    row 2:               | va va     vc vc
                                  vc vc        row 1:  v1 v1  v2 v2 | va va     vc vc
                                               row 0:  v1 v1  v2 v2 | va va     vc vc
              [conductor, 6.70 m away]                    [conductor, 3.37 m away]
```

**Measured in a scratch copy** (desk rows, without the wedge pass of rows 2 and 3):

| Input | `S` now | `S` with the fix |
|---|---|---|
| String sextet, "Full" | 14.29 m | 8.89 m |
| Bach-like file with nine string seats | 20.65 m | 14.26 m |
| Bach demo | 10.43 m | 11.58 m. The third violins move behind the second violins |
| `perf-60.mid` | 13.67 m | 13.85 m |
| The other demos and the choir test file | unchanged | unchanged |

No footprints touch. No large species hides 10% of a smaller head.

- Layout tests: the stage side table (line 431: Bach), the test "puts the first desk of each
  string seat in row 0 and the other desks in row 1" (line 362), the 1.6 m test for the wedge
  rows (line 486), and the near-corner bound of `perf-60.mid`.
- Footprints: no new kind of footprint.
- Shots: the Bach demo changes its stage and its shot plan.
- Hidden heads: no change in the measured inputs.
- Design: 4.5, seat geometry and the rows table. The demo pipeline can keep the nine Bach string
  parts after the fix.

### F2. The principals sit at the wrong end of five sections (S1)

**Code.** A row takes its groups from left to right, and each group takes its players in the
order of the cast: player 0 first (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:1535`). Thus player 0 sits at
the left end of each section, on both sides of the conductor. Player 0 is the principal, and it
plays the highest note (design 3.8, `docs/design/03-midi-to-seats.md:369`).

**Practice.**

- In the strings, the principal sits in the outside chair, nearest to the audience (S10, S11).
  For the violins, the outside player sits at the right side of the stand. For the violas,
  the cellos, and the basses, the outside player sits at the left side of the stand (S12). In
  the layout, the outside chair is the chair farther from 0°.
- In the woodwinds, the principals sit together in the middle of the block. The Philharmonia
  plan reads "Piccolo, 2 flutes, 2 oboes, cor anglais" from left to right (S1). Thus the
  second players and the piccolo and cor anglais sit at the outer ends.
- In the horns, "the principal horn (first horn)" sits "on the right and fourth horn seated on
  the left", from the view of the director (S14).

**Effect** in Beethoven 9 with "Auto":

| Section | Principal now | Practice | Correct now? |
|---|---|---|---|
| First violins | -89.6°, outer | Outer | Yes |
| Second violins | -38.4°, outer | Outer | Yes |
| Violas | 12.2°, inner | Outer (36.5°) | No |
| Cellos | 60.8°, inner | Outer (89.6°), next to the conductor at the front edge | No |
| Flutes | -25.0°, left end | Right end, next to the principal oboe (0.5°) | No |
| Oboes | 16.4°, left end | Left end | Yes |
| Clarinets | -24.4°, left end | Right end, next to the principal bassoon (-12.2°) | No |
| Bassoons | 0.0°, left end | Left end | Yes |
| Horns | -48.9°, left end | Right end (-36.7°) | No |
| Trumpets, trombones | Inner end | Inner end | Yes |

The solo shot of a seat frames its principal (`src/shots/subjects.ts:473`). Thus the cello solo
shot frames the inside cellist, and the flute solo shot frames the outermost flute.

```text
 now:      flutes  P  .  .  | oboes P  .        cellos  ...  P(61°)   .(90°)
 practice: flutes  .  .  P  | oboes P  .        cellos  ...  .(61°)   P(90°)
           (piccolo outer)    (cor anglais outer)          (principal at the front edge)
```

**Fix.** Reverse the order of the players inside these groups: `viola`, `cello`, their back
desks, `flute`, `clarinet`, and `horn`. Give the shared stand by the desk number
(`Math.floor(player / 2)`) in place of the even player index (`sharesWith()` and
`standsAlone()`, `src/models/layout-scene.ts:325` to `:339`).

```typescript
/** The groups whose principal sits at their right end (fix of F2). */
const PRINCIPAL_AT_RIGHT: ReadonlySet<RowGroup> = new Set<RowGroup>([
  'flute',
  'clarinet',
  'horn',
]);

/** The groups whose principal sits at their outer right end, nearest the audience (fix of F2). */
const OUTSIDE_AT_RIGHT: ReadonlySet<RowGroup> = new Set<RowGroup>([
  'viola',
  'cello',
  'viola-b',
  'cello-b',
]);
```

**Measured in a scratch copy.** The stage sides of all demos, of the choir test file, of the
sextet, and of `perf-60.mid` do not change. No footprints touch. The hidden heads do not
change, because each section keeps its set of places.

- Layout tests: the stand test (line 728) and each test that reads player 0 of a seat at a
  place.
- Footprints: no change. The lone stand of an odd seat stays at the right of its player.
- Shots: the solo shots of the violas, the cellos, the flutes, the clarinets, and the horns
  move to the other end of their section. The shot statistics of design 5.6 change. Another
  agent works on `src/shots/` now, so this fix needs a merge with that work.
- Hidden heads: no change.
- Pose: the ripple of a lift starts at the principal (`src/pose/rest.ts:86`). It then runs
  from the outer end toward the middle for the right-side strings.
- Design: 4.2 (the strings and the woodwinds bullets) and 4.5 (seat geometry).

A note on the pitch rule: player 0 takes the highest note. When the piccolo plays in the flute
seat of Beethoven 9, the piccolo line takes player 0. F8 gives the limit of a seat with merged
staves.

### F3. With a harp and a keyboard, the woodwind block moves right (S1)

**Code.** Row 2 holds the keyboard, the harp, the flutes, and the oboes, and the row is centered
on 0° as one block (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:148` and `:459` to `:461`). Design 4.5 gives
this rule (line 1285).

**Effect** in Boulanger: the boundary of the flutes and the oboes is at +27°. The oboes reach
+59.9°, in front of the trumpets. The harp sits at -15.4°, near the middle, and the celesta at
-44.4°. The principal clarinet (-10.8°) and the principal bassoon (+11.0°) then do not sit
behind the principal flute and the principal oboe.

**Practice.** The woodwind block sits on the center line of the conductor. The Philharmonia plans
draw the center line between the flutes and the oboes and between the clarinets and the
bassoons (S1). The harp, the celesta, and the orchestral piano sit at the left, behind the
violins (S1 Salonen plan, S6).

```text
 Boulanger row 2 now:       celesta  harp  fl (0°) fl  fl | ob ob ob      (split at +27°)
 Boulanger row 2 with fix:  celesta   harp   fl fl fl | ob ob ob          (split at +1°)
 Boulanger row 3 with fix:   hn hn hn hn   cl cl cl | bn bn bn   tp tp     (split at +1°)
```

**Fix.** Center rows 2 and 3 on the boundary between the left woodwinds and the right
woodwinds, as rows 0 and 1 center on the boundary of the violins. The left side of row 2 holds
the keyboard, the harp, and the flutes. The left side of row 3 holds the horns and the
clarinets. Each side spans at most 80°. A row with no woodwinds on one side stays centered.

**Measured in a scratch copy:** in Boulanger, the boundary moves to +1° in both rows. The harp
moves to -41.3°, and the celesta to -70.4°, behind the first violins. `S` grows from 11.28 m to
12.64 m. The other demos, the choir test file, and `perf-60.mid` do not change. No footprints
touch. No large species hides 10% of a smaller head.

- Layout tests: the stage side of Boulanger (line 431), and the keyboard test (line 561), which
  still holds.
- Footprints: no new kind.
- Shots: the Boulanger shot plan changes. `src/shots/view.test.ts:101`,
  `src/scene/focus.test.ts:102`, and `src/scene/light-pool.test.ts:17` use 11.28 m as a fixed
  value. Check that they still describe the demo.
- Hidden heads: no change.
- Design: 4.2 (diagram and woodwind bullet) and 4.5 (rows table, Boulanger stage side).

### F4. The spread moves two stand partners 1.4 to 1.5 m apart (S2)

**Code.** The spread grows each arc of a row by the factor `k`, up to 1.6 m
(`src/models/layout-scene.ts:1286`). It also grows the arc between the two players of one desk.

**Effect** with "Auto": the stand partners of row 0 sit 1.49 m apart (violins) and 1.42 m apart
(violas). The packed arc is 0.95 m. The shared stand then stands 0.71 to 0.75 m to the side of
each player. The two cellos of a desk need 1.67 m before the spread, and they stay.

**Practice.** Two string players share one stand (S11). The pandemic tests of the Tokyo
Metropolitan Symphony Orchestra kept 90 cm between string players. European trials kept 1.5 m
and 2 m (S21). Thus the desks of the demos have the spacing of a distanced orchestra.

**Fix.** Keep the packed arc between the two players of one desk, and spread only the arcs
between the desks and between the sections.

**Measured in a scratch copy:**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beethoven 9 | 1.49 m, 1.42 m | 0.95 m | 0.223 S | 0.241 S |
| Beethoven 8 | 1.49 m, 1.46 m | 0.95 m | 0.191 S | 0.205 S |
| K. 525 | 1.21 m, 1.56 m | 0.95 m | 0.220 S | 0.308 S |
| `perf-60.mid` | 1.07 to 1.56 m | 0.95 m | 0.206 S | 0.231 S |

The cellos keep 1.67 m in all rows of the table.

The stage sides do not change. No footprints touch. No large species hides 10% of a smaller
head.

- Layout tests: the near-corner test (line 445). K. 525 at 0.308 S stays under the bound of

- Hidden heads: no change.
- Design: 4.5, the spread bullet and the near-corner values.

### F5. The winds, the brass, and the percussion sit on the stage floor (S2)

**Code.** All instrumental rows stand on the stage top at height 0 (`placeRow()`, called at
`src/models/layout-scene.ts:574` with no height). Only the choir has risers.

**Practice.** The Philharmonia plans put each row behind the strings on a riser: "Risers = 8"
minimum high intervals" and "All risers = 20 cm minimum height intervals" (S1). The Tonhalle
stage has steps, and the brass and the percussion can sit "right at the top" (S16).

**Fix.** Put row 2 on a riser of 0.2 m, row 3 on 0.4 m, and row 4 on 0.6 m. Raise the choir
risers by 0.6 m, so that the lowest choir level keeps 1.0 m above the back row.

**Measured in a scratch copy** (only the players move up, so the footprints of the stands and
the risers were not checked):

| Riser heights of rows 2, 3, 4 | Choir | Largest hidden head (large species) |

### F6. The harpist has no music stand (S2)

**Code.** `STAND_USE.harp` is `'none'` (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:197`). Design 4.5 gives
stands only to the strings and the winds (lines 1176 to 1181).

**Practice.** "Most harp players position their music stand just in front of the harp, as close
as the legs will allow" (S20).

**Fix.** Give the harp its own stand in front of the player, at the side of the column, with the
rule of the wind stands (`ownStandDistance()`).

- Layout tests: the stand test (line 728) and the overlap test.
- Footprints: one more stand footprint in row 2. The arc of the harp can grow.
- Shots: the harp solo shot shows the stand.
- Hidden heads: no effect expected. Not measured.

The timpani and the percussion also have no stand. The section "Unverified" lists this point.

### F7. The risers split one voice part across two levels (S2)

**Code.** `fitChoir()` fills the levels with `divideSingers()`, by count, in the order of the
seats (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:590` to `:595` and `:650` to `:655`). A voice part can thus
start at the right end of one level and go on at the left end of the next level.

**Effect** in a scratch file with an orchestra and three choir seats of 8 singers: the stage gets
two levels of 12 singers. The second voice part has 4 singers at the right end of level 0 and 4
at the left end of level 1.

```text
 level 1:  B B B B  C C C C C C C C        A, B, C: the three voice parts
 level 0:  A A A A A A A A  B B B B        B is split into two blocks
```

With two or four seats of 8 singers, the levels hold whole parts, as in the choir test file.

**Practice.** Seating by voice sections "may be the most common way to seat choirs" (S23). In
the block sectional formation, the singers of one part stand together in one block (S22).

**Fix.** Give each level whole seats when the counts allow it. Else put each seat in one column
across all levels, as the blocks of S22. Keep the rule of the smallest stage among these
arrangements.

- Layout tests: the riser test (line 783) and the choir stage side (12.18 m) if the levels change.
- Footprints: no new kind.
- Shots: the choir shots of design 5.6 frame the parts.
- Hidden heads: the choir test file must stay below 10%.

### F8. A wind seat shows its polyphony, so Beethoven 9 shows 2 of 4 horns (S2)

**Code.** A woodwind or brass seat gets one player for each simultaneous note
(`src/score/players.ts:224`, design 3.8, `docs/design/03-midi-to-seats.md:333`).

**Effect.** The Beethoven 9 score has four horn staves (`demos/groups/beethoven-9.json`, line 3,
"Hn 1" to "Hn 4"). The pipeline merges them into one channel (`demos/README.md`, line 109). In
the excerpt, the horns play at most two notes at a time, so the stage shows 2 horns. In the same
way, the piccolo plays in the flute seat.

**Practice.** A symphony orchestra has 4 horns (S19, S1 with 5 horns). The finale of the
symphony uses four.

**Fix.** Let the demo pipeline write the staff count of each wind group, for example in the
track name, and use it as the smallest player count of the seat. For files of the user, keep
the polyphony rule. This is a choice of the user, because it adds players with no notes of
their own.

- Layout tests: the Beethoven 9 stage side, because row 3 gets two more horns.
- Shots and hidden heads: measure again after the change.

### F9. A full violin section with a piano counts as a chamber group (S2)

**Code.** `chamberFormOf()` counts the players of all seats (`src/models/cast.ts:106`). With the
setting "Full", the violin and piano duo test file gets 4 violinists and a pianist. That is 5
players, so the layout makes an arc with no conductor.

**Practice.** A chamber group has one player for each part. Four violinists on one part are a
section, and a section plays with a conductor or a leader in an orchestra layout (S11).

**Fix.** Count the players that "One per part" gives for the chamber rule (`onePerPart` in
`planSeat()`, `src/score/players.ts:224`). Keep the decision of the user: at most 5 parts give a
chamber group.

- Layout tests: the chamber tests (lines 941 to 1046) with "Full".
- Footprints, shots, hidden heads: only the "Full" layouts of the chamber test files change.

### F10. The contrabasses sit in a line at the back (S3)

**Code.** Row 4 holds the percussion, the contrabasses, the timpani, the trombones, and the tuba
(`src/models/layout-scene.ts:150`). The user chose this on 2026-10-05, so that the hippos hide no
smaller animal (design 4.2).

**Practice.** "Double basses will usually be behind the cellos" (S2). The Berlin Philharmonic
puts them "on the left or the right behind the strings" (S15). The Vienna Philharmonic and the
Tonhalle-Orchester put them in a line at the back (S2, S16).

**Verdict.** A valid variant. The research report (section 7) measured that the basses behind the
cellos hide 10% to 12% of the head of a cellist. No change is proposed.

### F11. Divisi gives the upper note to the front desks (S3)

**Code.** `divide()` gives each note a block of players in order (`src/score/players.ts:419` to
`:428`). With 4 violins and two notes, players 0 and 1 (the first desk) play the upper note.

**Practice.** The usual rule gives the upper note to the outside player of each desk. A second
technique gives the upper note to the front desks (S13).

**Verdict.** A valid variant. If the user wants the usual rule, give the upper note to the even
players (the outside players) and the lower note to the odd players. Then a pair of stand
partners plays two notes, as in a real section.

### F12. The choir risers are deeper and higher than choral risers (S3)

**Code.** Each level is 0.8 m deep and 0.25 m higher than the level in front of it. The lowest
level is 1.0 m high (`src/models/props.ts:82` to `:105`, design 4.5 line 1123).

**Practice.** Choirs "sing on risers with eight inch elevations and individual rows 12 - 18
inches in depth" (S22): a step of 0.2 m and rows of 0.30 to 0.46 m.

**Verdict.** A valid stylization. The lowest level of 1.0 m keeps the heads of the penguins
above the back row (design 4.2). The deep levels hold the penguins and their folders. No change
is proposed.

## Unverified

No source of high trust confirmed these points. Each item gives the steps to check it.

1. **The timpani and the percussion have no music stand** (`src/models/layout-scene.ts:207` and
   `:208`). Professional timpanists and percussionists read from stands, as photos of
   orchestras show. The audit found no text source. To check: find a stage plan or a
   percussion method that places the stand, then apply the fix of F6 to these sections.
2. **The order of the wind quintet** (`CHAMBER_ORDER`, `src/models/layout-scene.ts:160` to
   `:183`). The test file gives flute, oboe, horn, clarinet, bassoon from left to right. The
   audit found no standard order. To check: compare the stage photos of three professional
   wind quintets.
3. **The piano trio** (the arc form of design 4.2). The code puts the piano at the left end of
   the arc, with its case along the arc, and the violin and the cello to its right. Many trios
   put the piano at the back and the strings in front of its curved side. To check: compare the
   stage photos of three professional piano trios with `layoutScene()` of a trio file.
4. **The vocal soloists on the lowest riser level** (design 4.2 and 4.5). Performances of
   choral symphonies put the soloists in front of the choir or beside the conductor. The audit
   found no source that gives the more common place. To check: count the soloist places in
   videos of the Beethoven 9 finale by three major orchestras.
