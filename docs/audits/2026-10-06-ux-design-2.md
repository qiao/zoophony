# Audit: end-to-end UX and design, second round

Date: 2026-10-06. Auditor: an agent in the role of a senior product designer and UX auditor. The
auditor did not build the UI and did not change app code. The first audit is
[`2026-10-05-ux-design.md`](2026-10-05-ux-design.md). Its finding ids (FI1, SC1, and so on) stay
the same here. The new findings have ids with the prefix N.

## Scope and method

- Code: `main` at `2a72c99`. The file and line numbers below are for this commit.
- Build: `pnpm build`, then `pnpm vite preview` on port 4402. `pnpm size` gave 255,334 B.
- Browsers: Playwright 1.63 headless Chromium with `--use-angle=metal --enable-gpu
  --ignore-gpu-blocklist`, Google Chrome 154 (channel `chrome`) with the same flags, and a short
  run in Playwright WebKit and Playwright Firefox. No run used `--autoplay-policy`. A Playwright
  click or tap is a user gesture.
- Sizes: 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080, 1280 × 720, a phone of 390 × 844 with touch and device scale 3
  (also in landscape, 844 × 390), and a tablet of 820 × 1180 with touch (also 1180 × 820).
- Reduced motion: `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` for a play of 20 s and a change to Cinematic.
- Network: no throttle, 50 Mbit/s with 20 ms, and slow 3G (0.4 Mbit/s with 400 ms), with the CDP
  network throttle.
- Camera: one screenshot in the middle of each of the 175 shots of the six demos, at 1440 × 900
  and at 390 × 844 (350 frames). The shot plan comes from `prepareShots()` with the seed of the
  app. The sheets in this folder label each frame with its shot number, start, kind, and subject.
- Journeys: the first 22 s of the default demo, 32 s of each demo from the start, a demo switch,
  the drawer and the phone sheet, the keys, the tooltips, a drag and a wheel, the song end, the
  load failure, the demo file failure, a text file, and the page with no WebGL2.
- Files of the auditor, made with `src/midi/test-smf.ts`: `piano-solo.mid` (one piano, 51 s, the
  sequence name "Morning Study") and `violin-concertino.mid` (a solo violin on channel 1 at
  velocity 112, string ensemble, viola, cello, flute, and horn, 50 s, no usable sequence name).
  Both were dropped on the page with a synthetic drag and drop.
- Sound: an `AnalyserNode` on each channel of each node that connects to the destination. The
  script read the RMS level and the peak every 60 ms to 100 ms.
- Frame time: one run of `node tools/measure-frame.ts` (Cinematic mode, `perf-60.mid`).

Severity: **P0** broken, **P1** clearly unpolished, **P2** polish, **P3** nice to have. Size:
**small** (less than a day), **medium** (one to three days), **large** (more).

All images are in [`2026-10-06-ux-design-2/`](2026-10-06-ux-design-2/).

## Summary

**Verdict: a polished player with one weak area, the composition of the close shots.** The first
10 seconds are now right: the page names the app, the wide stage holds through the count-in,
and the first cut comes at 10 s. The load states, the tooltips, the keys, the drawer, and the
phone sheet work and look consistent. The frame time, the mute, and the demo switch are fast. 34
of the 38 findings of the first audit are fixed, and 4 are partly fixed. No finding regressed.
The largest remaining problem is in the film. Many close shots have a large object of another
player in the foreground: the black case of the celesta or the harpsichord, a head at the frame
edge, or the bear on a phone. The soloist place of AM3 added the piano case to the shots of the
first violins.

**Polish score: 7 of 10.** Clean close shots (no foreign object over 15% of the frame) would
raise it to 8.5.

**Top 10 fixes**, in order of value for the cost:

1. **N1 (P1)**: keep the soloist and the piano case out of the close shots of the first violins.
2. **N2 (P1)**: reject a close shot with a head of another player at the frame edge.
3. **N12 (P2)**: keep the messages clear of "Select a piece" on the first screen.
4. **N13 (P2)**: style the seek and volume sliders in Firefox and WebKit as in Chrome.
5. **N6 (P2)**: show a wide or high shot at least every 60 s.
6. **N4 (P2)**: limit the steep top-down close shots that show the dark void beside the stage.
7. **N14 (P2)**: move the large Play button off the stage on a short landscape screen.
8. **N3 (P2)**: test the foreground of a close shot with the phone aspect.
9. **N10 (P2)**: give a solo line on its own channel one player at the soloist place.
10. **N8 (P2)**: make the singers of the choir read as singers in a close shot.

## New findings

### Stage and camera

#### N1. The soloist place puts the piano case and the soloist in the violin shots (P1)

- **Seen:** The celesta (Boulanger) and the harpsichord (Bach) now stand at the front left, in
  front of the first desk of the first violins. Each solo shot of the first violins shows the
  black lid over 30% to 45% of the frame, with the head of the pianist over its edge
  (`shot-boulanger-violin-behind-celesta-1440x900.webp`): Boulanger frames 5, 18, and 25, and
  Bach frames 6, 21, and 27. The section shots of the violins show the lid in a lower corner:
  Boulanger frames 8 and 20, and Bach frames 3 and 24 (`shots-boulanger-1-16-1440x900.webp`,
  `shots-boulanger-17-32-1440x900.webp`, `shots-bach-bwv1048-1440x900.webp`). In K. 315,
  the standing flute fills the lower right of the violin shots 11 and 17
  (`shots-mozart-k315-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The first violins carry the melody in most pieces. The largest shape in
  their shots is a black slab.
- **Cause:** The clearance test of `src/shots/clearance.ts` checks the sight lines to the head,
  the hands, and the instrument. The foreground rule of `src/shots/foreground.ts:51` checks only
  heads. A large box beside the sight lines passes both tests. The soloist place comes from
  `src/models/layout-scene.ts:58` (AM3).
- **Fix:** Add a screen-share test for each close camera: project the boxes of the other players
  and of the piano case, and reject a camera when they cover more than 15% of the frame. For the
  first violins, search the azimuths on the side away from the soloist first. Change design 5.6
  ("Clearance" and the foreground rule). Size: medium.

#### N2. Heads of other players fill the frame edges of many close shots (P1)

- **Seen:** A large head or ears of another player stand at the lower edge or a corner in at
  least 12 of the 175 desktop frames: Boulanger 7, 10, 11, 12, 22, 23, and 30
  (`shot-boulanger-tuba-foreground-head-1440x900.webp`: a panda head is larger than the tuba
  player), Beethoven 9 frame 10, Beethoven 8 frames 5 and 16, and K. 315 frames 8 and 26. The
  first cut to the horns at 18.8 s has a head cut by the lower edge
  (`shot-boulanger-horn-top-down-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The eye goes to the large near head. The subject looks like a background
  player.
- **Cause:** The foreground rule tests a head only when it is in the middle area of 0.4 × 0.6 of
  the frame (`src/shots/foreground.ts:21` and `:26`, design 5.6). A head at the edge passes.
- **Fix:** Test the full frame. Reject a camera when a foreign head nearer than 0.75 of the
  subject distance shows more than 25% of its sphere in the frame. Keep the middle-area rule for
  heads that are farther away. Change design 5.6. Size: small.

#### N3. On a phone, the conductor and the case fill the foreground of close shots (P2)

- **Seen:** At 390 × 844, the shots of the first violins show the head and the baton of the bear
  over the lower third, and the case of the celesta on the left. The rabbit is small in the top
  half (`phone-shots-boulanger-390x844.webp`, frame "18 solo violin"). Many other phone close
  shots are crowded in the same way (`phone-shots-boulanger-all-390x844.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The portrait frame shows more of the near area. The phone gets the weakest
  version of the film.
- **Cause:** The foreground rule reads a desktop aspect of 1.6 (design 10, the table of the
  camera and stage findings). The conductor is not in the list of heads.
- **Fix:** Run the rule of N1 and N2 for the aspect 0.46 as well, and add the conductor to the
  heads. When no camera passes, use a longer lens (a smaller field of view) for the phone. Change
  design 5.6. Size: medium.

#### N4. Close shots of the back rows look straight down and show the void beside the stage (P2)

- **Seen:** The horn, percussion, and woodwind shots look down at about 40° to 60°. The animals
  show the tops of their heads, and the dark area beside the stage fills 30% to 45% of the frame:
  Boulanger 2, 15, 17, and 21, and the first cut to the horns
  (`shot-boulanger-horn-top-down-1440x900.webp`). Beethoven 8 shows the woodwinds three times
  from almost straight above (frames 1, 13, and 21 of `shots-beethoven-8-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The faces are the charm of the animals. A top-down frame hides them and
  reads as a security camera.
- **Cause:** The elevation of a section or solo shot rises with `back` (`18 + 24 × back` and
  `14 + 26 × back`, design 5.6), and the clearance search moves the camera higher and to the
  side to clear the front rows.
- **Fix:** Limit a close shot to 30° of elevation. When no camera clears the front rows at that
  height, use a longer lens over the front rows, or a section shot of the front rows. Add a
  penalty for a frame with more than 25% of background beside the stage. Change design 5.6.
  Size: medium.

#### N5. The cuts fall on a fixed grid in pieces with a steady tempo (P2)

- **Seen:** In Bach, the 34 middle shots last 9.6 s, 4.8 s, or 7.2 s, and 30 of them last
  exactly 9.6 s. In K. 525, all shots last 7.3 s, 10.9 s, or 14.5 s (`plans` of
  `prepareShots()`).
- **Why it matters:** A cut every 9.6 s for five minutes feels mechanical. A concert film cuts
  with the phrase, and phrases differ.
- **Cause:** The phrase bonus of +1.0 for a bar index that is a multiple of 4
  (`src/shots/prepare.ts:83`) is larger than the cost of the difference to the wanted length
  (1 for each 3 s).
- **Fix:** Give the phrase bonus only to a phrase line with an entry or a change of the lead, or
  lower it to 0.5. Check that each demo then has at least 6 different shot lengths. Change design
  5.6 ("Cut points"). Size: small.

#### N6. Some demos show no wide view for two to three minutes (P2)

- **Seen:** K. 315 has no wide or high shot from 10 s to 183 s (173 s). Boulanger has none from
  114 s to 232 s (118 s). Beethoven 8 has only a high shot at 33 s between 11 s and 125 s.
- **Why it matters:** The viewer loses the full stage, which is the image of the product, and
  the soloist place of AM3 is never seen in context.
- **Cause:** Rule 3 of design 5.6 sets a minimum gap between wide shots, and no rule sets a
  maximum.
- **Fix:** Add a bonus to the wide and high candidates that grows after 45 s with no wide view,
  so that one comes at least every 60 s. Change design 5.6 ("Choice of the next shot"). Size:
  small.

#### N7. The same framing repeats (P2)

- **Seen:** Beethoven 8 shows "section oboe, clarinet" three times with the same top-down
  framing (11 s, 109 s, 185 s). K. 315 shows the solo flute six times from almost the same angle
  (frames 1, 4, 10, 13, 15, and 18).
- **Why it matters:** A repeat of a frame looks like a loop.
- **Fix:** Keep the last azimuth of each subject in the plan, and start the search of the next
  shot of that subject on the other side, at least 20° away. Change design 5.6. Size: small.

#### N19. After a drag in a close shot, the camera stays inside the orchestra (P3)

- **Seen:** A drag of 60 px in the celesta shot turns the view to the stage center, as SC6 asked.
  The camera stays 4 m from the center, among the stands, and a second drag shows the backs of
  the stands (`drag-in-close-shot-and-switch-1440x900.webp`, upper row). A long wheel motion
  brings the stage back.
- **Fix:** At the change to Free mode from a close shot, also move the camera out to at least
  0.6 of the default distance over the same 0.4 s. Change design 2.5. Size: small.

### Animals and motion

#### N8. The singers of the choir do not read as singers (P2)

- **Seen:** In the Ode to Joy, the penguins hold their folders and barely move over 6 s. The
  beak motion does not show at 1440 × 900 (`beethoven-9-singers-6-frames-crop.webp`). The
  penguin face is a white mask under a black cap with no visible eyes. The four vocal soloists
  stand in the choir row and look the same as the choir, so the baritone recitative shows a row
  of identical penguins (`shots-beethoven-9-1440x900.webp`, frames 1 and 7). The section shot of
  the choir is good (`shot-beethoven-9-choir-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The choir is the reason for this demo. The other animals have clear eyes
  and charming faces.
- **Fix:** Give the penguin eyes on the white mask (design 4.3, 4.5). In a close shot of a
  singer, open the jaw more and add a small lift of the head on long notes (design 5.3). Put the
  vocal soloists in front of the choir, beside the soloist place of 4.2. Size: medium.

#### N9. A piano solo gets a conductor, a large empty stage, and shots of the bear's back (P2)

- **Seen:** `piano-solo.mid` shows the piano and the bear in the front left corner of a stage
  that is mostly empty. The film alternates between the pianist and the back of the bear
  (`file-piano-solo-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** A piano piece is a common file. A conductor for one pianist looks wrong,
  and the shots of the bear's back are the weakest frames of the film.
- **Fix:** For a score with one seat, hide the conductor and the podium, put the soloist at the
  stage center, and use a smaller stage. Use no conductor shot with fewer than 3 seats. Change
  design 4.2, 4.5, and 5.6. Size: medium.

#### N10. A solo violin on its own channel plays as a full section (P2)

- **Seen:** In `violin-concertino.mid`, the track "Solo Violin" (velocity 112, above the
  orchestra) becomes "Violins 1, 4 rabbits". The string ensemble becomes "Violins 2, 4 red
  foxes". No player stands at the soloist place (`file-violin-concertino-1440x900.webp`,
  `file-violin-concertino-seats-1440x900.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The violin concerto is the most common concerto. Four rabbits play the
  solo line in unison.
- **Cause:** `src/score/soloist.ts:118-124` accepts a seat only when it has one player and no
  other seat has its section. "Auto (Full)" gives the solo seat a full section first.
- **Fix:** Find the soloist before the section size: a seat whose track name holds "solo", or
  a seat that is above the orchestra by the rule of 3.8 while it shares its section. Give it one
  player and the soloist place. Change design 3.8. Size: medium.

### Controls and UI

#### N12. A message covers "Select a piece" on the first screen (P2)

- **Seen:** The message box sits 84 px above the bottom (`src/ui/ui.css:724`). The intro of the
  first screen ends at the same height (`src/ui/ui.css:148`). The failure of the instruments
  hides "Select a piece" fully. The refusal of a text file covers half of it
  (`message-over-intro-crop.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** "Select a piece" is the way out of a failure. The message hides it.
- **Fix:** While the first screen shows, put the message above the large Play button, or hide
  the intro while a message shows. Change design 2.8 ("Messages"). Size: small.

#### N13. The sliders look different in Firefox and WebKit (P2)

- **Seen:** Chrome shows a gold thumb on a dark track. Firefox shows a gray native thumb and a
  bright white seek track across the bar. WebKit shows white pill thumbs, and the speed menu has
  an up and down arrow (`bar-sliders-chrome-firefox-webkit.webp`).
- **Why it matters:** The bright track of Firefox is the strongest line on the screen during
  play. Design 1 lists Firefox and Safari as supported browsers.
- **Cause:** The sliders set only `accent-color` (`src/ui/ui.css:339-350`).
- **Fix:** Style `::-webkit-slider-runnable-track`, `::-webkit-slider-thumb`, `::-moz-range-track`,
  `::-moz-range-progress`, and `::-moz-range-thumb` with the tokens of 2.8, and set
  `appearance: none` on the speed menu with an icon "collapse". Change design 2.8 (tokens).
  Size: small.

#### N14. On a short landscape screen, the large Play button covers the stage (P2)

- **Seen:** At 844 × 390, the button center is 160 px from the top, in the middle of the
  orchestra (`phone-landscape-first-844x390.webp`). At 1280 × 720, it touches the front edge of
  the stage (`first-screen-1280x720.webp`).
- **Cause:** `top: min(72%, calc(100% - 230px))` (`src/ui/ui.css:148`). The rule of 230 px wins
  on a short screen.
- **Fix:** Below a height of 500 px, put the button and the intro at the right of the stage, or
  hide the intro line and use 72% with a smaller margin. Change design 2.8 ("First screen").
  Size: small.

#### N17. The speed menu and the volume slider have no tooltip (P3)

- **Seen:** A hover of 650 ms shows no tooltip on these two controls. The other controls of the
  bar have one.
- **Fix:** Add "Speed" and "Volume". Change design 2.8 ("Tooltips"). Size: small.

#### N18. A click on an animal moves the stage away from the pointer (P3)

- **Seen:** A click on a rabbit opens the drawer at "Violins 1". The canvas then moves 176 px to
  the left, so a second click at the next animal misses (`click-on-animal-1440x900.webp`).
- **Fix:** When a click on an animal opens the drawer, keep the canvas in place until the next
  pointer leaves the stage, or move it only when the drawer opens from the bar. Change design
  2.8. Size: small.

#### N20. A seat with one player has a plural name (P3)

- **Seen:** The K. 315 soloist row is "Flutes, 1 tan fox" (`click-on-animal-1440x900.webp`).
- **Fix:** Use the singular for a seat with one player ("Flute"), and "Solo flute" for the
  soloist (`src/app/seat-text.ts:14`). Change design 2.8 ("Seat texts"). Size: small.

#### N21. The title shade shows as a dark box over a light floor (P3)

- **Seen:** In close shots over the wooden floor, the shade behind the title is a visible dark
  rectangle with soft edges (`shot-boulanger-horn-top-down-1440x900.webp`, top left).
- **Fix:** Use a radial gradient from the corner, or lower the alpha to 0.6 where the contrast
  test still passes. Change design 2.8 ("Title"). Size: small.

### Loading and files

#### N15. On a slow network, the bar shows "0:00 / 0:00" over an empty page (P3)

- **Fix:** Hide the length and the seek bar until the file of the piece arrives
  (`src/ui/bar.ts:176`). Size: small.

#### N16. The load ring jumps back when the first byte arrives (P3)

- **Seen:** The turning part covers 15% of the ring. At the first byte, the ring changes to
  about 1% (stroke offsets 213.6 then 248.4 of 251.3).
- **Fix:** Keep the turning part until the byte share passes 15%
  (`src/app/play-control.ts:177`). Size: small.

#### N22. The default demo needs 160 s on slow 3G, with no hint of the time (P3)

- **Seen:** The 17 preset files of Boulanger (9.4 MB) loaded at 160 s on slow 3G. The text
  counts files ("9 of 17" at 127 s) and gives no size or time.
- **Fix:** Add the size to the load text after 10 s, for example "Loading instruments: 9 of 17
  (5.1 of 9.4 MB)". Change design 2.8 ("Message texts"). Size: small.

#### N11. The count-in of a slow piece delays the first sound by 3 s (P3)
