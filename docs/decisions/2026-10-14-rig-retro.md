# Decisions of the rig retro of the form and neck rounds

The Section column names a section of the design. [`design.md`](../design.md) gives the file of
each section.

On 2026-10-14, after the merge of the tan flute fox, the manager of the session chose the rig
items of the retro of the form round and of the neck round, and four items of the retro of the
flute round (user rule of 2026-10-12). The agent of the branch `retro-rig` decided the rows below.
The report [`2026-10-14-rig-retro.md`](../research/2026-10-14-rig-retro.md) gives the times and
the measures. The user can reverse each decision. Then update the section that holds it, and
remove its row.

| Decision | Section |
|---|---|
| `.gitattributes` merges `docs/design.md` with the union driver of git, so that two rounds that each add the line of a decision file do not conflict. The test in a temporary repository gave no conflict for a merge and for a rebase with the driver, and a conflict for both with no driver (`docs/research/2026-10-14-rig-retro/union-merge.sh`). The risk: a line that both sides change stays two times, once in each form, with no conflict. The merge script stops on no such line, so the agent of a round that changes a line of `docs/design.md` reads the merged file | 9.1 |
| A hold round of a body starts only after each form or scale round of the same body has merged, and the implementation brief of `docs/briefs/studio-body.md` has a section "Dependencies" that names each such round. The flute hold of the tan fox needed a second fit on the final fox form. The brief template holds the rule, because the manager plans the order of the rounds | 9.8 |
| Before a fit, the agent checks that the pose code can show the rest pose and the hold of the source, and adds a missing mechanism first, with values that change nothing (section "Poses" of the implementation brief) | 9.8 |
| A test compares no value of one body with a value of another body. `AGENTS.md` ("Tests") and the template ("Tests") give the rule with the example of the manager, "the fox arm is shorter than the wolf arm" | 9.3 |
| A variant of `pnpm rig-mesh --check --set` keeps the meters for each unit of the base: the landmark height of `ANIMALS` and `app.shoulders` of the variant take the factor of `keptHeight()` of `tools/rig-mesh.ts`, the height of the standing shoulder joints of the variant over the height of the base, after the rise of the legs. The base keeps its settings, so its rig keeps the bytes of the GLB file. The copy of `tools/glb-swap.ts` takes the settings of the variant (`withGlbBody()`), so the app checks keep the meters too. A first run with the height of `ANIMALS` in the copy gave a fox of `legFactor=1.6` a head 2.6% smaller | 4.4 |
| The table of the variants gives the seat of the legs: the seat height of the export with no seat of `ANIMALS` (`legSettings()` of `tools/rig-mesh-check.ts`). The column `Seat m` stays the seat of `ANIMALS`, 0.36 m for the fox and the rabbit, so it does not change with a leg factor. The form round gave the seat of the legs of each form from the export with no seat | 4.4 |
| `tools/rig-form.ts` measures the form of each player of the animal in the copy of each variant and one time in a probe tree of `main` (`FORM_BASE`): the seat, the visible head (the corners of the head bone of the seated player, as `landmark-probe.ts` of the form round), the standing and the seated head tops, the footprint of the seated player on the support of its instrument, and the stage side of each demo. It imports only the code of the app and `tools/patch-demos.ts`, so it also runs on the tools of `main`. The commit is `main` with no option, because a rig round compares its variants with the merged bodies. The probe of `main` runs before the first rig, so only the stop of the probe tree is on during it. When it fails, the tables give no values of `main`, and the check goes on | 4.4 |
| The column of the bones of the folds gives, for each role, the 3 main bones (`FOLD_BONES`) of the folded faces with the most face-samples over the rest, the play, and the lift (`foldBones()`). The face table of `pnpm fold-probe` counts each face sample of each set too | 4.4 |
| `sameFaces()` of `tools/fold-probe.ts` pairs the folded faces of two trees with other corners by the distance of their middles, the nearest pair first, each face in one pair at most, within 1 cm. Of two pairs at the same distance, the lower index goes first. The greedy order keeps the pairs of the case of the review (tree faces 1 and 2, base faces 11 and 12), and it needs no assignment solver | 4.4 |
| The copy of `tools/glb-swap.ts` holds the configs of the mesh rigs, `tools/mesh-rigs/*.json` (`copiedTool()`), so that `pnpm hold-reach --size --glb <file>` gives the arm factor of the rig. The config path is not an argument of the child, because the child finds the config next to its script, as with no `--glb` | 4.6 |
| `rig_mesh.py --check` lists each face whose normal turns more than 45° from the source to the long-armed source, or whose shortest edge keeps less than half of its length (`stretch_faces()`), and each long thin face of the rule `check.thin` whose longest edge crosses the plane of a ring at an arm joint within `near` of the joint (`uncut_faces()`). A ring splits each edge that it cuts at that plane, so such a face has no ring. The lists give the index of the module, as `pnpm fold-probe` counts the faces (`module_faces()`), and at most 30 faces each. `pnpm rig-mesh --check` passes `--check` with `--obj`. The flag changes no byte of the rig | 4.4 |
| A ring of `refine.rings` with no `radius` takes `part` (by default 0.75, `RING_PART`) of the arm part that ends at its joint: the upper arm for the elbow, the forearm for the wrist, and the clavicle for the shoulder (`ring_radius()`). The arm factor stretches the upper arm and the forearm, so the radius grows with it. The ring of 0.13 units at each elbow of the rabbit is 0.75 of its upper arm with the arm factor 1.81. The rabbit keeps its `radius`, and the fox has no ring, so both rigs keep the bytes of `main` with no edit of a config. The round `arms-rest` of the same time owns the configs | 4.4 |
| The comment above each entry of `ANIMALS` in `tools/studio-body.ts` is one line that names the decision files of the entry. The reasons are in the rows of these files. The rows of the scale of the rabbit and of the red fox in [`2026-10-14-fox-rabbit-form.md`](2026-10-14-fox-rabbit-form.md) now give the parametric landmark of each scale (the shoulder joints of the parametric rabbit at 0.581 m, the head top of the parametric fox at 1.083 m), which only the comments held | 4.4 |
| A probe tree of a commit (`withProbeTree()` of `tools/ref-tree.ts`) takes `tools/` of the commit with its source, and copies only the scripts of the working tree that the commit does not have, such as `tools/rig-form.ts` on a commit before this branch. The tools of a commit fit its source, so a branch whose `tools/studio-body.ts` imports a function that the commit does not have no longer stops the probe of the commit. The other side of this rule: the probe of the commit runs its own version of the script, so a change of the JSON of a probe shows only on a commit that has the change. `pnpm hold-fit fox-violinist --base 6350b207~1` ran on the tools of that commit | 9.3 |
| A probe tree can check the role of a probe before the probes start (`role` of `ProbeTreeOptions`, `checkRefRole()`): a child process imports the table of the roles of the commit and lists its keys, so that no code of the commit runs in the process of the tool. `pnpm hold-fit --base` checks `FOLD_ROLES` of `tools/fold-probe.ts`, and a commit with no such role gives "The ref <ref> has no role <role>" with the roles of the commit. A commit with no such table passes, and its probe gives its own failure. `pnpm fold-probe --base` and `pnpm pose-depth --base` can take the option, and this round does not own them | 9.3 |
