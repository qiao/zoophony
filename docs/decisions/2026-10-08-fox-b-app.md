# Decisions of the route B fox in the app

The Section column names a section of the design. [`design.md`](../design.md) gives the file of
each section.

On 2026-10-08, the agent of the route B fox in the app decided the details below. The user chose
route B of the fox spike ([`2026-10-08-fox-spike-gated.md`](../research/2026-10-08-fox-spike-gated.md)):
a fox from Blender with silhouette gates, a new skeleton of 23 bones, automatic weights with up
to 4 bones for each vertex, and flat face colors. The user wants to see this one fox play on the
stage before the other species. The user can reverse each decision. Then update the section that
holds it, and remove its row.

## Plan

The pose code finds the bones and the joint places as follows today:

- `buildBiped()` gives the bind position of each bone (`BodyBones.positions`) and the body
  points: the head center, the mouth, the palms, the finger blocks, and the floor.
- No bone has a rotation in the bind pose (design 4.7). The arms hang down, each palm faces the
  body, and the fingers point down.
- The pose code reads only these values. `armRig()` reads the shoulder, the elbow, and the wrist.
  The IK turns each arm bone from its bind direction. Each hold gives the world rotation of a
  hand from the hanging hand.
- The instruments and the bow get their bind places from the body points. The bow lies in the
  hanging right hand.
- The GPU binds the mesh at the bind positions, with no rotation.

The route B fox has its joints in the seated playing pose of `fox-views.png`. The left hand holds
the violin neck, and the right hand holds the bow at the chest. This plan drives it with the pose
code of today:

1. Map the 23 bones onto the old bone names. `tail.3` and `tail.4` join `tail2`. The eye
   triangles move from `head` to `eyeL` and `eyeR`, so that the eyes blink.
2. Make a rest skeleton in the old form. The trunk, the legs, and the tail keep the joints of the
   new rig. The arms hang straight down from the shoulder joints of the new rig, with the arm
   lengths of the new rig.
3. Calculate the bind pose of the mesh as a pose of this rest skeleton: the turns of the arm
   bones that move the hanging arms onto the arm joints of the new rig. Each bone gets a bind
   frame: a place and a rotation.
4. The GPU binds each body bone at its bind frame. The instrument bones keep their rest frames,
   because the instruments and the bow have their places in the rest skeleton.
5. Read the body points from the mesh: the head center, the mouth, the palms, and the finger
   blocks.

```text
fox.glb of route B
   │  pnpm pack-rigged <glb> fox-red
   v
src/models/rigged/fox-red.ts: joints in the sheet pose, colors, mesh with 4 bones
   │  buildRigged()
   ├──> rest skeleton (arms down) ──> poseAt(): IK, holds, idle ──> rotations ──┐
   │                                                                            v
   └──> bind frames (sheet pose) ──> scene: inverse bind matrices ──────> GPU skin
```

The other ways, and why this plan does not use them:

- **The new rig as the rest skeleton.** The holds turn a hanging hand, and the bow lies in the
  hanging palm. With the hands of the sheet pose in the bind pose, the hands and the bow turn to
  wrong places.
- **An unskin of the mesh to the hanging arms.** A skin with linear blends has no exact inverse,
  so the unskinned mesh changes its form.
- **A new pose layer for the new skeleton.** It repeats all holds of design 5.3, and the other
  species keep the old layer.

## Decisions

| Decision | Section |
|---|---|
| The red fox (`fox-red`) of the stage and of the views pages is the route B fox. All other species keep the parametric body or the sculpt of today | 4.4 |
| `pnpm pack-rigged <glb> <species>` reads the GLB file of route B from its path and writes `src/models/rigged/<species>.ts`. The packed module is in the repository, so CI needs no Blender. The module of the red fox comes from `docs/research/2026-10-08-fox-spike-gated/fox.glb` | 4.4, 7.1 |
| A packed position is a step of 0.25 mm. A packed weight is a step of 1/255: the four weights of a vertex add up to 1, the largest first, and the main bone takes the rest of the rounding. The packer welds the vertices of the color borders by their place and their skin | 4.4 |
| The 23 bones of the rig map onto the bones of the player with the same names. `tail.2`, `tail.3`, and `tail.4` map onto `tail2`, because a player has two tail bones. The eye triangles follow `eyeL` and `eyeR` with the weight 1, so that the eyes blink | 4.7 |
| Each corner of a rigged body follows up to four bones (`MeshData.skin`). `MeshData.bones` and `MeshData.blendBones` keep its two bones with the largest weights, so that the readers of the main bone keep their rules | 4.4, 4.7 |
| The rest skeleton of a rigged body has the joints of the rig for the trunk, the legs, the tail, and the shoulders. The arms hang straight down from the shoulder joints with the lengths of the rig, and the face looks to the front. The pose code of section 5 drives it with no change | 4.7 |
| The mesh binds at bind frames: the smallest turns from the hanging arm onto the arm joints of the mesh, the hand along the hand bone, and the 12° turn of the face. The instrument bones keep their rest frames | 4.7 |
| The head center of a rigged body has the height of the head center of the skeleton of the species (2.05 H above the seat for the red fox), between the eyes, halfway from the eyes to the back of the head. At the height of the eyes, the bow hand went 0.04 H into the muzzle | 4.4, 4.7 |
| A rigged body takes the stage colors of the sculpt of its species: the nearest role of the palette of route B gives the color and the finish of each material | 4.5 |
| A rigged body fits each seat with the drop of 0.35 m of its sheet. On another support, such as the stand of a violin soloist, the body is parametric, because the model has no standing legs | 4.4 |
| The sheet page turns the head of a rigged body as its mesh, which has the pose of the sheet. The stage and the page of `animals.webp` turn it to the front | 7.1 |
| The bow arm of the red fox reaches 0.285 m of the bow hair from the frog, a little more than half. The strokes map into this range, and the rig keeps its joints | 5.3 |
| In the tests, a point is in a part of a rigged body when the nearest triangle of the closed shell belongs to the part.
