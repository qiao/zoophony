# Decisions of the tan flute fox

The Section column names a section of the design. [`design.md`](../design.md) gives the file of
each section.

On 2026-10-14, the user said: "now replace the fox by @~/Downloads/fox.glb", and chose "All foxes,
violins first". The red violin foxes got the mesh first
([`2026-10-14-mesh-fox.md`](2026-10-14-mesh-fox.md)), and the user then asked for a fox of 1.3
times the size ([`2026-10-14-animal-size.md`](2026-10-14-animal-size.md)). This round gives the
same rig to the tan flute foxes. The manager of the session and the agent of the branch
`fox-flute` decided the rows. The report
[`2026-10-14-fox-flute.md`](../research/2026-10-14-fox-flute.md) gives the measures and the images.
The user can reverse each decision. Then update the section that holds it, and remove its row.

| Decision | Section |
|---|---|
| The manager: the tan foxes (`fox-tan`) take the studio body of the red foxes, the fox mesh of the user in its final form (`2026-10-14-fox-rabbit-form.md`, `2026-10-14-neck-violin.md`), with a tan palette of the same faces (`SpeciesParams.palette`). The tan fox shares the entry `fox` of `ANIMALS`, so it takes the head, the neck, the legs, the arms, and the chair of the red fox. One rig and one module serve both foxes. A second module of the same mesh adds about 26.6 KB after gzip, and the palette adds 461 B to the module of the fox | 4.3, 4.4, 4.5 |
| The tan palette is the map of `colors.py` of the measure round of the fox mesh (`paletteMap()` of `tools/studio-palette.ts`): the Oklab color of each matte face moves by the steps from the fur, the belly, and the accent of the red fox to the colors of the tan fox, blended by the nearness of the face to each red color, with a share that falls to 1/e at the Oklab distance 0.08. The hue step of the fur shrinks with the chroma of the face, so that a face of low chroma does not turn green. The gloss faces of the eyes and of the nose keep their colors. `pnpm studio-body fox` writes the palette from `AnimalSettings.palettes` | 4.4, 4.5 |
| The manager: the pose target is the hold of a real flutist. Michel Debost, "Debost's Comments", Flute Talk, May/June 1996: "a flutist will have to turn his head laterally about 30 degrees", which is better than to push the right elbow out of line with the shoulder, and seated, "elbows and shoulders should be low". The flute lies square to the face, with a small slope down to the right | 5.3 |
| A wind hold can turn the head to the left while the player plays (`WindHold.headTurn`). The neck and the head turn as the look turns them (`NECK_SHARE` of `pose/frames.ts`), and the mouthpiece moves with the turned mouth. The head is square to the chest in the rest pose. Only the tan fox has a head turn | 5.3 |
| The tan fox turns its head 30° to the left, the turn of Debost. On the final form, the right palm reaches its grip with 6.2 cm to spare, the play folds in 0.7% of the samples, and each depth stays within 1.5 cm. On the form of the size round, 30° folded three faces of the neck and the head, and the round took 25° | 5.3 |
| A species can hold a wind instrument in its own way (`SPECIES_WIND_HOLDS`, `windHoldOf()` of `pose/winds.ts`), as a species can hold a bowed string. The tools change such a hold with `--set 'flute@fox-tan.<path>=<value>'` | 5.3 |
| The lip plate of the tan fox sits 0.05 H below the mouth at the front of the snout, under the long muzzle (`WindHold.lipShift`). At the mouth, the jaw touches the flute at each head turn from 20° to 30°, and 0.05 H lower, the head stays 0.6 cm from it. On the form of the size round, the lip plate sat 0.3 H back and 0.25 H down, because at the mouth five faces of the shoulders folded. The longer arms of the final form removed these folds | 5.3 |
| The shoulders stay square: the right shoulder has no forward turn (`reach` 0). The left elbow points down and out, (-1, -1, -0.3), and the right elbow out to the side, (1, -0.8, 0). The left elbow pole does not change the folds: the left arm is almost straight | 5.3 |
| The rest pose of a real flutist: Patricia George, "Solving the Problem of Rests", The Instrumentalist, December 2018: the flute stands "vertically on the right leg with the right hand on the right-hand keys". On the final form, the flute upright on the higher right thigh puts the left upper arm 2.5 cm to 3.2 cm into the body. So the tan fox keeps the rest of the first fit: the flute leans from in front of the chest down to the right knee, with the foot joint down, and the right upper arm goes 1.0 cm into the body. The wind pose keeps both palms on their grips, so the left paw does not go to the lap. In the rest pose, the left elbow points out to the side, (-1, 0, 0) | 5.2, 5.3 |
