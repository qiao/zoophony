# Decisions of the round retro-arm

The Section column names a section of the design. [`design.md`](../design.md) gives the file of
each section.

The retros of the rounds `rabbit-arm` and `cat-trunk` of 2026-10-16 gave four slow steps. The
search of a rest hold of the rabbit took 40 minutes, 10 of them in scans that built the scene of
each demo again for each variant. The final probes ran three times, because a new column of
`pnpm hold-fit` on `main` stopped each `--base main`. The zoomed views of the armpit needed a
preview script of their own. In the round `cat-trunk`, a `--grid` could not go on top of a `--set`
variant, and two runs of `--base main` probed `main` two times. The manager asked for five tool
changes, and the agent of the branch `retro-arm` decided these rows.

| Decision | Section |
|---|---|
| `pnpm hold-fit <role> --scan` builds only the rig of one seated player again for each variant (`rebuiltRig()` and `scanRest()` of `tools/lap-rest.ts`, a bowed string or a wind instrument), as `rest-scan.ts` of the round `rabbit-arm` did. The layout and the other players stay, because no layout rule reads a hold. The strokes and the wind tracks keep the old rig, and the rest pose before the first play does not read them. The roles with a lap rest (a violin, a viola, or the flute of the tan fox) can scan, and the other roles stop with a message | 9.3 |
| The scan measures one rest sample by default: the first seated body of the role in the first demo of the run (`bach-bwv1050` for the rabbit), at the wall time 21.1 s (`SCAN_WALL`), the time of `rest-scan.ts`. `--walls` takes more wall times, and `--demo` takes the first seated body of each named demo. Each row gives the folded faces of the studio skin by side and the cells of the rest columns of a run, for one variant and one sample. On the rabbit of `bach-bwv1050` at the four wall times of `REST_WALLS`, the largest values of the scan equal `probeLaps()` with the base and with one variant of the right arm, and the folds equal the rest row of `pnpm fold-probe` | 9.3 |
| With a grid, the scan sorts the variants by the goals that they miss (the cells with the mark `OVER` over their samples), then by their folded faces, and the rows of one variant stay together. The scan takes up to 1000 variants of the grids (`SCAN_GRID_LIMIT`): one sample of the rabbit takes about 0.03 s to 0.04 s. It goes with none of `--base`, `--json`, `--faces`, `--sets`, and `--step`, because it has one sample set, no sweep, and no compare | 9.3 |
| The 101 variants of a grid 10 × 10 of `violin@rabbit.restBow.lap.along` (0.55 to 1) and `violin@rabbit.restBow.lap.offset.0` (0.1 to 1) took 5.6 s in the scan with one sample, and 14.9 s with the four samples of `REST_WALLS`. The same variants as 100 `--set` options with `--sets rest --demo bach-bwv1050` took 69.7 s. A run with all sample sets on `FIT_DEMOS` takes about 70 s for one variant (`AGENTS.md`), so the 100 variants would take about 2 hours | 9.3 |
| With `--set` and `--grid`, each `--set` variant takes the product of the grid ranges on top of it, and the changes of the grid come after its own changes. The run has no row of a `--set` variant alone, because a grid usually holds the value of the variant. A run with no `--set` puts the grids on top of the base, as before. `GRID_LIMIT` (40) holds for all variants of the grids on all `--set` variants | 9.3 |
| `probeBeside()` of `tools/ref-tree.ts` keeps the JSON text of the commit in `probe-cache/<key>.json` in the common folder of git, which each worktree shares, beside `check-cache/` of `tools/check-commits.sh`. It is a folder of its own, because each file of `check-cache/` is the mark of a passed check, and a JSON text of a probe is another kind of entry. The key holds the entries of `PROBE_PATHS` of the commit in `git ls-tree`, the scripts of the working tree that the probe copies, the arguments of the commit, Node, and the lockfile of `node_modules`. A docs commit on `main` thus keeps the key. Nothing removes old entries: remove the folder by hand. `PROBE_NO_CACHE=1` runs the commit again | 9.3 |
| `pnpm hold-fit`, `pnpm pose-depth`, and `pnpm fold-probe` turn the cache on, and `pnpm shot-trace` does not, because its run takes less than 1 s. The two sides run at the same time, so the cache removes the CPU of the commit and the extraction of its tree, and the wall time drops only when the commit is the longer side. With two variants of the violinist, the CPU fell from 27.7 s to 17.1 s, and the wall time stayed 15 s | 9.3 |
| The JSON text of a probe gives the field `since`: the oldest format version that its version only adds fields to (`ProbeFormat` of `tools/ref-tree.ts`). A change of a field takes the next version and sets `since` to it. An added field takes the next version and keeps `since`. Two versions compare when `since` of the newer version is the older version or lower (`formatNote()`), and a newer working tree gives `-` for each new field of the commit. A newer commit shows the fields of the version of the working tree. A text with no `since` has `since` equal to its version, so the probes with no `since` (`pnpm body-probe` and `tools/rig-form.ts`) stay strict | 9.3 |
| `FIT_SINCE` is 6: the version 6 removed the measures of the bow tip and changed the gap of the lower end of a violin, and the version 7 only added the right arm (`AddedLaps` of `tools/hold-fit.ts`). `ROWS_SINCE` and `FOLD_SINCE` equal their versions. The field `since` takes no new version, because it changes no field of the rows. `pnpm hold-fit violinist --sets rest --demo bach-bwv1050 --base 56704f33~1` (the version 6) compared in 3.7 s, with `-` in the four columns of the right elbow of the commit | 9.3 |
| `pnpm close-views species --zoom <n>` takes the camera nearer to the middle of the box of the body, as the query `zoom` with `body` of the gallery and as `zoom-shot.ts` of the round `rabbit-arm` did. The gallery of `src/` has no target height for a camera around the body, and the round does not change `src/`. A zoom of 2 to 2.5 shows the armpits and the lap of a seated player. `--elevation <deg>` gives each view one angle above the floor, from 0° to 89°, the limits of the gallery. The cells keep their size (360 × 450) and their file names, and the label of each cell gives the zoom and the elevation. `batch` gives both options to its species parts, and both need `--species` | 9.8 |
