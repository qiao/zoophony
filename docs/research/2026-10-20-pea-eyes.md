# The bent pea eyes of the studio bodies

Date 2026-10-20. Round `pea-eyes`, from `main` 5025a90d. Decisions:
[`2026-10-20-pea-eyes.md`](../decisions/2026-10-20-pea-eyes.md). The probes and their outputs are
in [`2026-10-20-pea-eyes/`](2026-10-20-pea-eyes/). Each command runs from the root of the tree.

## 1. The question

The user saw the pea eye of the rabbit stand out of the cheek like a disc on edge, and the bear
eyes far from the head. The goal: the rim of each oval at the fur in each view, the front of each
eye out of the fur in the front view, and the same size, place, and happy look.

## 2. The cause

`eye-fit.ts` reads the modules of the studio bodies and measures each eye against the faces of
its head. A flat oval does not fit the studio heads, for two reasons:

- The head curves under the oval. The rabbit cheek falls 0.022 H at the sides of the oval and
  0.033 H at its top and bottom, from the plane at its middle (the fit terms `uu` -5.2 and `vv`
  -3.8 to -4.1). So the rim stood 0.027 H to 0.030 H out, though its axis is only 3° to 9° from
  the normal.
- The direction of an eye differs from the normal of the surface by 3° to 24°, and by 44° on the
  bear. The tilted oval stands out at one edge. `eyePush()` then moved the bear eyes 0.046 H out,
  and the hippo eyes 0.029 H to 0.033 H.

## 3. The measures

`node docs/research/2026-10-20-pea-eyes/eye-fit.ts scratch/main-studio` (the modules of 5025a90d)
gives `eye-fit-main.log`, and the probe on the tree gives `eye-fit-bend.log`. The columns:

- Rim out: the largest distance of a rim corner of the oval out of the head (its corners within
  0.012 H of the plane of the middle, before the bend).
- Rim to face: the angle between a plane fit of the rim corners and a plane fit of the head
  surface at their places.
- Axis to normal: the angle between the direction and the mean normal of the faces within 0.09 H.
  The round keeps each direction.
- Front corners in: the corners more than a quarter of the depth in front of the middle that lie
  inside the head (the rule of `eyePush()`).
- Seen: the area of the eye that a ray from an orthographic camera reaches before the head, at 0°
  (the front), 60°, and 120° to the side of the eye, in H².

## 4. The variants

`eye-fit.ts --variants` with `BEND=1 WS=0,0.5,1` turns the axis from the normal (w 0) to the
direction (w 1), with a flat oval and with a bent oval (`variants-bend.log`). A turn alone does not
fit the rabbit: along the normal, its rim is 0.024 H to 0.040 H out. Each bent oval puts the rim
within 0.001 H to 0.011 H of the fur at each w, so the round keeps the directions.

`LIFT` moves a bent eye out along its axis, in H. With the middle 0.01 H under the surface
(`LIFT=-0.01` on the tree, `eye-fit-under.log`), the rims lie 0.000 H to 0.007 H out, but only
64% to 89% of each eye shows at 0° and 60°, and the facets of the head tear the rabbit eyes
(`rabbit-under.webp`, `main` above that variant). With the middle on the surface
(`eye-fit-bend.log`), 86% to 96% shows, and the outline is clean.

## 5. The bear

The face under the bear eyes faces 31° to 48° up and twists: the `uv` term of the fit is 3 to 7
in each place within 0.08 H of the eye (`eye-places.ts`, `eye-places-bear.log`), and 9.2 under the
eye along its direction. The other bodies have at most 2.5. `bear-variants.webp` shows the
variants in the front, 60°, and side views, in the order of the table. The variant "bent, along
the direction" has the light of a flat eye, because it was drawn before the light rule of 4.5.

| Variant | Rim out, H | Look |
|---|---|---|
| `main`: flat, along the direction, moved 0.046 H | 0.076 | clean ovals, far from the head |
| bent, along the direction | 0.013 | sheared beans |
| bent, along the normal | 0.015 | beans |
| bent with no `uv` term, w 0.5 | 0.019 | beans: the muzzle cuts the inner lower part |
| flat, w 0.5, moved 0.003 H | 0.027 | clean ovals, near the head |

The numbers are in `bear-variants.log`. A flat oval at w 0.3, moved in until its front corners
meet the head, has its rim 0.018 H out, but only 64% of it shows from the front. No drawn variant
has a clean oval with a rim of 0.02 H or less, so the round stops on the bear, and the bear keeps
`main` (`flatEyes`).

## 6. The result

Each other studio body takes the bend, with the middle on the surface. Each rim lies 0.009 H to
0.016 H out, against 0.012 H to 0.072 H on `main`, and the rim lies within 1° to 4° of the face.
No front corner lies in the head. The sizes, the places, and the directions keep their rules, and
the light of a bent eye lies on its front. `*-pairs.webp` show `main` above the tree at 2x, for the
role of each body: the front, 60°, and the side. `lineup-front.webp` and `lineup-front60.webp`
show each studio species (`pnpm close-views species ... --views front,front60 --moment rest`),
`lineup-pairs.webp` the lineup of `pnpm close-views lineup`, and `rabbit-back-pairs.webp` the
rabbit from 120° behind its left side at 4x, as in the view of the user: the gap under the rim is
gone. The faces keep their happy look.

Open: in the front view, the bent mouse eyes lean in at the top (`oboist-pairs.webp`). The surface
under them faces 17° to 21° more up than their direction (`variants-bend.log`), and the bend shears
the oval in a view across that direction.
