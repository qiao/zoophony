# A dual quaternion bind of the arm roots

Date 2026-10-22. Round `dq-bind`, base `d3046531`. Decisions:
[`2026-10-22-dq-bind.md`](../decisions/2026-10-22-dq-bind.md). The probes and their outputs are in
[`2026-10-22-dq-bind/`](2026-10-22-dq-bind/). Each command runs from the root of the tree. Copy a
probe of `probes/` to `scratch/` before you run it. `dump-mat.ts` and `decomp2.py` are in
[`2026-10-22-rabbit-armpit/probes/`](2026-10-22-rabbit-armpit/probes/), `flap.ts` in
[`2026-10-22-bear-armpit/`](2026-10-22-bear-armpit/), and `dump.py` in
[`2026-10-22-arm-roots/`](2026-10-22-arm-roots/).

## 1. The question

The user found dents and flaps at the arm roots of many animals, and asked "why there are dents
everywhere?" and "or is the dent due to twisting?". The rabbit armpit round put 84% of the depth
of the rabbit dent on the bind step, and named the linear blend as the cause. The user chose:
"Yes, test the bind". The test: a bind step with dual quaternion skinning, which keeps the radius
of a vertex at a bent joint, behind the rule `bind.blend` of the rig config (`bind_skin()` of
`tools/blender/rig_mesh.py`). The measure that decides: the depth of the arm root vertices below
their neighbors, in the bind and in the play, base against flag.

## 6. The result

The dual quaternion bind does not shrink the dent of the rabbit after five variants, so the round
stops by the rule of the brief, and tests no other species. The flag stays in the code with no
config that uses it. The dent at the arm root comes from the bend itself. The fixes that remain
change the form: the source copy at the root (a fill or a push), or a bind arm nearer to the
hanging source arm (`studio.bindArms`), which makes the bend smaller.
