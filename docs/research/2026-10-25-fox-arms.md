# The rebuilt arms of the foxes and the flute grip

Date 2026-10-25. Round `fox-arms`. Decisions:
[`2026-10-25-fox-arms.md`](../decisions/2026-10-25-fox-arms.md). The probes and their outputs are in
[`2026-10-25-fox-arms/`](2026-10-25-fox-arms/). Each command runs from the root of the tree. `main`
is bbcbec18.

## 1. The question

The fox mesh serves the red fox violinist and the tan fox flutist with one rig, and the rig had
only the four old arm bones. The wind arm round measured a twist step of 63.7° on the left
forearm of the tan fox, and the palm grip of the flute twisted the right paw 142° and flipped. The
round rebuilds the arms with the method of `rig-blender.md`, keeps the accepted violin grip of the
red fox, tries the palm grip of the flute again, and moves the rest flute higher in the paw. The
arm gate, the grasp of `pnpm wind-grip`, the depths, and the 2x cells decide.

## 2. The faults on `main`

`views-fv-main.webp` and `views-fl-main.webp` hold the cells of both roles on `main`. The red fox
shows jagged dark facets on the top of the right shoulder from the front (tip, front), a dark wedge
at the right inner elbow in the play from az35 (frog, az35), and dark shards at the side of the
trunk in the side views of each moment (frog, tip, lift50, right). The tan fox shows a dark slit at
the right inner elbow and spiky flaps on the top of both shoulders at rest (rest, front), and a dark
band under the left forearm in the play (note, az35). In the play, neither paw grasps the flute, and
at rest the right paw holds the flute near its lower end, with the foot joint beside the knee.

## 3. The route

The fox upper arm joins the trunk down to about 0.6 of its length. A square cut finds a closed
loop only from there, so its bridge ran down the trunk wall and turned 9 to 20 faces over in the
seated bind, at each tilt and cut share. The session therefore takes the route of the cat
(`tools/blender/fox_arms.py`): a seam cut at |x| 0.1, a side wall under each arm, and a tube with a
flat inner side.

| Try | Result |
|---|---|
| Square cut at 0.55 to 0.7 of the upper arm, tilt 0° to 60° | 9 to 86 faces turned over in the bind |
| Seam cut, two shoulder rings, rule weights on the first ring | gate folds 26 to 41 on the right arm |
| Seam cut, Blender weights on the first ring | right folds 25 (red fox), 17 (tan fox) |
| Collapse of the thin bridge faces at the socket | 0 faces turned over in the bind, red fox right folds 14 |
| Chest share 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6 near the side walls | red fox right folds 11, 9, 11, 10 |
| Collapse with the armpit line too, two shoulder rings | lower twists, and a dark slit at the back of the arm root |
| No shoulder ring | each arm folds 2 or 3 faces, no slit (chosen, `slit-pairs.webp`) |
| Skin normals within 0.12 or 0.15 units of the shoulder joints | no slit and a softer wedge at the back of the red fox right armpit from the stage camera (0.12 chosen, `root-normals.webp`) |

`variants.txt` gives the gate rows of each try. The rule weight of each upper arm goes to its first
segment (`armSegments.rootSegment`), so that the socket rolls with the first ring of the tube. The
fingers keep half of their Blender weight: with the whole weight, the red fox paw on the violin
neck moved from 5.3 cm (`main`) to 7.0 cm off the neck axis, and with half 4.4 cm.

## 4. The flute

`pnpm wind-grip flutist --set` scans gave the grips (`grip-scan.txt`): the left paw 0.2 H off the
tube toward the player with a curl of 115°, and the right paw 0.1 H past the axis and 0.14 H
across with a curl of 106°. Each paw grasps in each play sample (0% on `main`).

At rest, the right paw first held the flute with the roll of the play. Then the paw turned 175° in
one frame at the end of the lift: it pointed at the tube, and the direction to the tube square to
the hand lost its length (0.005). The rest grip with the roll of the shoulder (`restAim` 0) holds
the tube in the closed paw with no flip. The grips of the play and the rest lie on two sides of the
tube, so on a straight line the grip crossed the axis: `WindHold.gripAround` moves it around the
tube. A roll center for `faceTube()` and a fade of the roll did not remove the turn and are not
kept.

## 7. The result

Both foxes take the rebuilt arms. Open: the twist steps stay above the target of 25°, because the
paw twists of the holds are large (168° on the red fox violin hold of the user). The red fox
forearm goes 1.7 cm into the body after the last note of `boulanger-matin`, and its upper arms
2.3 cm to 2.9 cm, under `main` but over the limit of 1.5 cm.
