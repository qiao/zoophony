## 3. The palm of the bow grip

The first try kept the bow grip 0.02 H from the paw on the line through the middle of the paw.
The stick at the grip then kept 0.017 H to 0.019 H from the posed paw, but the frog end of the
stick went 0.013 H into the paw at 50 s of the default demo. The back of the slim paw bulges to
the palm side, behind the grip.

The rule now measures the whole stick:

```text
rest skeleton, right paw seen from the front (the stick runs along z, into the page)

          wrist
            |
        +-------+
        |  paw  |)  <- the bulge of the back of the paw
        |   x   |  .  <- stick: moves in from outside until it keeps GRIP_GAP
        +-------+       from the forearm and the hand, at the height of the grip
            x = middle of the paw (the finger block)
```

1. The skin of the arm bones moves the forearm and the hand to the rest skeleton (the sum of
   the four bones of each corner, as the GPU does).
2. Seen along the stick, each triangle is a flat triangle, and the stick is a point. The search
   moves the point in from outside the paw until it keeps `GRIP_GAP` (0.02 H) from the
   triangles.
3. The grip is the point of the stick across from the nearest point of the paw. Thus the gap at
   the grip is the smallest gap of the stick.

The left palm keeps the old rule. On the rule of the stick, it came 0.004 m under the strings
(limit 0.005 m). With the paws on the hand bones (section 4), the stick keeps 0.020 H from the
right paw at each moment of the tests. With the weights of bone heat, it moved from 0.019 H to
0.033 H, because the nearest vertex had a forearm weight of 0.17.

## 5. The full bow

A longer arm alone did not reach the tip: with the arm of 0.441 m and the hold of the stage, the
fox reaches 0.03 m to 0.37 m. A violinist with short arms brings the shoulder forward at
the tip and lets the bow slant. The pose code now does the same for a short arm (`TipHelp` in
`pose/bowed-rig.ts`, design 5.3):

```text
bow place on the free hair:   frog 0 ------------------ 1 tip
shoulder turn (violin):       20° ------------------> 20° + turn
bow slant (violin):          -20° ------------------> -20° - slant
help (turn, slant):          (0, 0) -- grows evenly --> full help
```

```typescript
/** The help of the shoulder and of the bow slant toward the tip, for a short bow arm. */
export interface TipHelp {
  /** The extra forward turn of the right shoulder bone at the tip, in radians. */
  readonly turn: number;
  /** The extra turn of the bow in the plane of the strings at the tip, in radians. */
  readonly slant: number;
}
```

`bowedRig()` scales the largest help (30°, 20°) in 10 even steps, and keeps the first step with
which the arm reaches the full hair. The help fades out in pizzicato. The rabbit, the cat, the
dog, and the hippo reach the full hair alone and get no help, so their poses do not change.

| Fox | Tip help | Bow range |
|---|---|---|
| Spike fox, rig of `main` | 27°, 18° | full hair |
| Final fox, right arm 0.441 m | 15°, 10° | full hair |

A turn of the torso does not help, because the violin rests on the chest and turns with the
shoulder. A search of the bow range with the elbow direction of the pose and three rolls of the
hand gave the rabbit and the cat a help of 3° too, so the search keeps the elbow direction of the
frog. At the end of the range on the lowest string, the posed arm of the final fox uses 97.4% of
its length (98.2% for the spike fox), and 85% to 94% on the other strings.

## 7. What remains

- **The rested violin has a small margin.** The rebuilt fox keeps the violin 0.021 m from the
  torso (bound 0.02 m). The low-poly thigh top under the tail end of the violin moves by up to
  0.016 m from one remesh to the next. A new thigh, as on the branch `fox-planes`, can fail the
  test again. A rest place at 0.9 of the way to the knee gave 0.031 m with the mesh of
  `fox-b-model`, but the hold of the violin moves the violins of all species. A rest place for a
  rigged body is the cleaner fix.
- **The end of the bow range** lets the arm use a little more than the 97% of design 5.3 (97.4%
  for the final fox), because the search uses the elbow direction of the frog.
- **The face planes** of the final fox are smaller than those of `fox-b-model` (2.41 faces for
  each plane, 3.57 before).
- **The hanging bow of the rest skeleton** is part of the footprint. It is never on the stage, but
  it sets the front of the box of the red fox, and the stage sides move with the right paw.
