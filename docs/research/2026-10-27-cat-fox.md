## 1. The questions

The user said: "the cat's arm has dent and is longer than expected" (the cat violist from behind
and to the left), and "the fox doesn't seem to be holding the instrument properly" (the tan fox
flutist from the front left). The gate, the depths, the grips, and the sheets decide.

## 2. The cat arm: the causes

**The dent.** `probes/pose-obj.ts`, `probes/obj-views.py`, and `probes/obj-ids.py` draw the posed
skin with its edges and face indices from the angle of the user. At rest and in the play, the
lower back of the left arm root folds into a crease: the tube faces 1453 to 1457 lie over the
faces of the socket and the armpit line (2506, 2510, 2511), with dark slivers between them
(`cause.webp`). The armpit line hangs half on the trunk and half on the arm (`faces.txt`). The
seated bind turns the hanging arm of the mesh 53° up onto the bind arm of the other meshes, so the
bind already bends this socket, and the crease angle 35 splits its normals into hard dark lines.
The user mesh has no dent there (`frontal.webp`).

**The length.** The rig keeps the arm of the mesh (`armFactor` 1): the upper arm is 0.219 m and
the forearm 0.223 m in each pose (`probes/arm-len.ts`), and the gate gives a stretch p95 of 0.06.
The shoulder joint lies 0.024 units below the top of the shoulder (`frontal.webp`). The sheet
`cat-views.png` gives 1.10 H from the shoulder to the wrist, and the rig 1.30 H. At rest, the left
paw held the viola on the front of the knee, and the elbow opened to 131° (`arm-len-main.txt`).

## 5. The flute hold: the cause

`pnpm wind-grip flutist` measures each paw on the skin. The round gives the flutist the places of
the technique: the middle of each paw in front of the flute and a little below, with the backs of
the paws to the audience (`ROLES` of `tools/wind-grip.ts`, wind-technique 2.4). On `main`, no paw
grasps the flute in the play (`wind-grip-main.txt`):

- The left paw hangs 5.5 cm below the axis of the flute, and its finger tips stop 2.7 cm from it.
  A paw took the roll and the aim of its tube only when it stays on its grip at rest
  (`poseSide()` of `src/pose/winds.ts`). The left flute paw leaves its grip at rest, so in the
  play it pointed at the flute with no roll, from a grip 0.2 H off the tube.
- The right paw lies on the top of the flute from behind, 3.1 cm toward the player, 170° from
  its place, with its fingers over the front.

The bind of the left paw of round `fifth-bind` did not move the grip: before that round, the left
tips stopped 3.3 cm from the flute (`wind-grip-67eeb20f.txt`).
