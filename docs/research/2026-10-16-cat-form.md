# The cat form: the taller chair, the shorter and wider trunk, and the shorter neck

Date 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17. Branch `cat-form`. Decisions:
[`2026-10-16-cat-form.md`](../decisions/2026-10-16-cat-form.md). The probes, their outputs, and the
images are in [`2026-10-16-cat-form/`](2026-10-16-cat-form/). Each command runs from the root of the
worktree. `var.py`, `bvar.py`, and `tree.sh` ran from `scratch/` of the worktree, with the path of
the worktree in their text, and they write `scratch/var/` and `scratch/trees/`: copy them there to
run them again.

The user asked for a taller chair with legs more perpendicular to the floor, a shorter and less
skinny cat, and a shorter neck (the quote is in the decision file). The round had two phases: the
variants with a mid-report, then the choice of the manager, the rig, the holds, and the docs.

## 3. The girth rule

`girth_moves()` of `tools/blender/rig_mesh.py` moves each vertex of the trunk bones and of the
clavicles away from the trunk axis, the line through the hips joint and the neck joint. The part of
its distance across the axis along x takes the width factor, and the part in the middle plane takes
the depth factor. Each arm moves as a whole with the move of its shoulder joint, each leg with its
hip joint, and the tail with its root. The neck joint lies on the axis, so the neck and the head do
not move.

```text
          axis (hips joint -> neck joint)
              |
   arm  <--  shoulder joint moves by its own distance across the axis
              |    trunk vertex: across = rel - (rel . axis) axis
              |    moved = p + (width - 1) * across_x + (depth - 1) * across_middle
   leg  <--  hip joint moves the same way
   tail <--  root moves the same way (back with the depth)
```

The clavicles widen with the trunk, because they hold the tops of the shoulders, and each arm moves
with its shoulder joint. The clavicle joint lies near the axis, so an arm that moved with it, as the
trunk factor moves the arm, would stay inside the wider shoulder. With no factors, the cat, fox, and rabbit rigs keep their
bytes, and [1.3, 1.3] gives the bytes of a single factor 1.3. `girthRise()` of `tools/rig-mesh.ts`
gives the rise of the shoulder joint by the depth, so that `keptHeight()` keeps the meters for each
unit: with [1.08, 1.15], the shoulder joint is 0.710786 units high, as the log of Blender gives it.
