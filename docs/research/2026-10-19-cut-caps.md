# The normals of the cut caps

Date 2026-10-19. Round `cut-caps`, base `b4a04918`. Decisions:
[`2026-10-19-cut-caps.md`](../decisions/2026-10-19-cut-caps.md). The probes and their outputs are
in [`2026-10-19-cut-caps/`](2026-10-19-cut-caps/). Each command runs from the root of the tree.

## 1. The question

The user said: "the arms for hippo and elephant seem twisted and deformed". The side view of the
hippo still showed a dark slot at the right armpit and light slivers at the right shoulder. The
light probe of the arms round found that the slot is the shading of the caps of the arm `cut`.
Their normals point into the gap between the arm and the trunk, away from the light. The panda,
the beaver, and the elephant have the same cut. The goal is a side view with no dark slot and no
light sliver. The measures are the black pixels of the close views, the play folds, and the
depths.

## 2. The caps

`studioNormals()` gives each corner of a listed cap the mean normal of the skin faces at its
vertex. `pnpm rig-mesh <animal> --check --keep <dir>` wrote each variant, and
`pnpm close-views <role> <dir> --glb <file> --base b4a04918` rendered it. `blacksum.py` counts
the black pixels (Oklab lightness below 0.3) of each view, summed over the moments, inside the
pixels that change (`black-pixels.txt`). "All" gives the rule to each new face of the cut: the
caps of the arm, the caps of the body, and the two quads at the top. "Arm" gives it to the caps
of the arm only. "All" came from a temporary change of the rig rule, and "arm" is the rule of
`cut.skinNormals`.

| panda | 37, 15 | 6,995 | 8,123 | 7,051 | right view: 2,115, 3,548, 2,183 |
| beaver | 42, 20 | 7,815 | 7,249 | 7,645 | right view: 2,183, 2,442, 2,123 |

| elephant | 48, 25 | 9,895 | 4,729 | 4,849 | back view: 6,564, 1,330, 1,507 |

The sums do not show the form of a mark. The images show it:

- `hippo-variants.webp`: with "arm", the slot at the front of the right armpit gets lighter and
  loses its sharp edges, and a soft shadow stays. With "all", the white caps of the belly turn
  into a dark wedge with light streaks under the arm.
- `panda-variants.webp`: with "all", the white caps under the right arm turn into a dark wedge
  (the right view goes from 2,115 to 3,548). The caps of the arm are black fur, so "arm" changes
  nothing that shows.
- `elephant-variants.webp`: both variants light the black caps of the back view. With "all", a
  black strip shows under the left arm in the left view, and "arm" keeps that view as the base.
- `beaver-variants.webp`: with "all", the right armpit gets darker. "Arm" keeps it, and at zoom 2
  the slot under the left arm gets lighter.

So the rule takes the caps of the arm only (`cut.skinNormals`), for the hippo, the elephant, and
the beaver. The panda keeps the normals of its faces. The skin corners do not count the caps in
their mean, so the rule changes no normal of the skin.

## 3. The slivers

`sliver-probe.ts` poses the bassist over all demos (play, step 0.5 s) and gives for each face the
largest angle between its normal and the normal of a face across one of its edges, and the share
of its smallest height that stays from the bind. A face with an angle above the crease angle of
35° shades flat, as a streak. In the bind, each of the four faces has angles under 30° to its
neighbors (`middles.ts` gives the source middles).
