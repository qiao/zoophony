Date 2026-10-27. Round `rigs3`. Decisions:

## 1. The question

The round `fifth-legs` left two faults. One face of the right inner elbow of the deer harpist
folded in 8.2% of the play samples, and from straight behind one soft dark face stayed on the left
calf of the polar bear pianist. The goal is the cause of each fault with a measure, and a fix with
no fold in any set, no new fold, and no worse arm gate or view than `main`.

## 2. The deer elbow

### The cause

`pnpm fold-probe harpist --faces 10` gives face 509 at the front of the right inner elbow, with the
corner angles 34°, 113°, and 33° (`fold-faces-main.txt`). It folds when the forearm turns 40° to
60° from the bind (`bins-509.txt`), at most 104° at 279.63 s (`turns-509.txt`). The weights of
its corners are smooth, and the mean of them folded the face in each sample (round `fifth-legs`).

`probes/elbow.ts` gives the neighbors of the face. Faces 509 and 475 are one quad of the tube, and
the two corners across their edge sum to 198.4° in the bind: the edge is the long diagonal.
`probes/elbow_quads.py` lists the quads near each elbow inside the rig (`elbow-quads.txt`). In
the source, the corners across that edge sum to 158.6°, and in the upright bind 197.1°. The bind
bends the elbow about 79°, and it shears the quad. So the thin face of the long diagonal turns
over when the elbow bends further (`elbow-cause.webp`, red).

### The variants

`probes/elbow.ts` counts the folds of mesh variants in memory over the four sets: the rest, the
play at steps of 0.25 s, the lifts, and the lowerings of `pnpm arm-check` at steps of 0.05 s.

| Variant | Rest | Play | Lift | Lowering | Faces that fold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `main` | 0% | 8.3% | 0% | 0% | 509 (93), 1951 (1) |
| flip 509/475, the long diagonal | 0% | 0.1% | 0% | 0% | 1951 (1) |
| flip 509/508 | 0% | 0.1% | 0% | 0% | 1951 (1) |
| flip 509/473 | 100% | 98.8% | 100% | 100% | 509 |

Face 1951 is a crease face of the left socket, with the same sample on `main`. The rule of the
knees (`legFlip`) tests the source, where the quad is good, and its sum of the squared edge
turns grows with the flip. A rule of each bad diagonal of the bind near an elbow would flip about
30 quads (`elbow-quads.txt`). So `point_flips()` of `tools/blender/rig_mesh.py` flips only the
edge at a named point (`refine.pointFlip` of `deer.json`), when its corners sum to more than 180°
in the bind.
