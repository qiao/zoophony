# The tan flute foxes with the fox mesh (2026-10-14)

On 2026-10-14, the user gave the fox mesh `assets/models/fox/fox.glb` and chose "All foxes, violins
first". The red violin foxes got the rigged mesh first
([`2026-10-14-fox-rig.md`](2026-10-14-fox-rig.md)), and the user then asked for a fox of 1.3 times
the size ([`2026-10-14-animal-size.md`](2026-10-14-animal-size.md)). This round gives the same rig
to the tan flute foxes (`fox-tan`), with a tan palette of the same faces and the flute hold of a
real flutist. The decisions are in [`2026-10-14-fox-flute.md`](../decisions/2026-10-14-fox-flute.md).

The fox changed its form two times during the round: the larger head, the longer legs, and the
higher chair ([`2026-10-14-fox-rabbit-form.md`](2026-10-14-fox-rabbit-form.md)), then the short
neck and the longer arms ([`2026-10-14-neck-violin.md`](2026-10-14-neck-violin.md)). The tan fox
shares the entry `fox` of `ANIMALS`, so it takes each form. The sections "The real hold" and "The
first fit" give the measures on the form of the size round. The section "The fit on the final
form" and the tables after it give the measures of the final hold, with the logs in `final/`.

The folder [`2026-10-14-fox-flute/`](2026-10-14-fox-flute/) holds each probe, log, and image of
this report. Run a probe from the root of the repository, such as
`node docs/research/2026-10-14-fox-flute/real-hold.ts`. H is the head height of the fox mesh at

## The real hold

The pose target is the hold of a real flutist:

- Michel Debost, "Debost's Comments", Flute Talk, May/June 1996
  (<https://www2.oberlin.edu/faculty/mdebost/may96.html>): "a flutist will have to turn his head
  laterally about 30 degrees", which is better than to push the right elbow out of line with the
  shoulder. Seated, "elbows and shoulders should be low".
- The Babel Flute, "Common Posture Mistakes", after Onorio Zaralli, "Playing with the soul"
  (<https://thebabelflute.com/?p=234>): the head turns to the left by about 40°, the shoulders stay
  parallel to the line of the feet, and the right shoulder does not move forward.
- The flute lies square to the face, across the lips, with a small slope down to the right. The
  sources give no angle of the slope, so the probes take 10° (the bind slope of the app flute is
  8°).
- The rest of a real flutist: Patricia George, "Solving the Problem of Rests", The Instrumentalist,
  December 2018 (<https://theinstrumentalist.com/?p=5640>): the flute stands "vertically on the
  right leg with the right hand on the right-hand keys".

`real-hold.ts` turns the mouth of the seated tan fox around the neck by the head turn, puts the
lip plate at the turned mouth, and lays the flute square to the face. It gives the distance from
each shoulder joint to its grip against the arm of 0.490 m, and the flute scale at which the grip
is at the arm.

| Head turn, slope | Left shoulder to grip | Right shoulder to grip | Right palm | Log |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0°, 10° | 0.461 m | 0.431 m | 5.9 cm to spare | `real-hold.txt` |
| 20°, 10° | 0.430 m | 0.465 m | 2.5 cm to spare | `real-hold.txt` |
| 30°, 10° (Debost) | 0.414 m | 0.485 m | 0.5 cm to spare | `real-hold.txt` |
| 40°, 10° (Zaralli) | 0.397 m | 0.504 m | misses by 1.4 cm, scale 0.90 | `real-hold.txt` |

Before the size round, the arm was 0.377 m, and with the head turn of 30° the right palm missed
its grip by 3.5 cm (`real-hold-before-scale.txt`). On the final form, the arm is 0.604 m, and with
the head turn of 30° the right palm reaches its grip with 6.2 cm to spare, and with 40° with
4.3 cm (`final/real-hold.txt`). The flute keeps its size, because the palms reach their grips in the
real hold of Debost.

`fold-grid.ts` moves the flute in its playing turn over a grid of places in the rest pose
(`fold-grid.txt`, `fold-grid-left.txt`). Faces 637 and 638 fold at each place where the left paw is
to the right of the left shoulder, and none fold with the flute 0.6 H to the left of the middle
and 0.3 H nearer to the body. Each real flute hold puts the left paw to the right of the left

of the neck folds. The muzzle stays 2.4 cm from the flute in each play sample
(`pose-depth-flutist.txt`, `--gaps`).

## The fit on the final form

On the final form, the play of the first fit folds in 0.7% of the samples: the longer arms of the
neck round, with the stretch of the upper arm outward from the shoulder joint only, removed the
folds of faces 637 and 638 in the play (`final/fit-final-1.txt`, `pnpm hold-fit flutist`). So the
lip plate goes back to the mouth, and the head turn goes to the 30° of Debost.

With the lip plate at the mouth, the head touches the flute at each head turn from 20° to 30°.
