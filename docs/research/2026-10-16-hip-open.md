# The seated posture of the red fox and the rabbit: upright trunk, sloped thighs, short level seat

Date 2026-10-16. Branch `hip-open` on `main` at `241bf704`. The decisions are in
[`2026-10-16-hip-open.md`](../decisions/2026-10-16-hip-open.md). The probes, the logs, and the
images are in [`2026-10-16-hip-open/`](2026-10-16-hip-open/). Two agents worked on the round: the
first one made the turn of the trunk of the rig and the fix of the standing body, and the second
one made the thighs, the seats, the crease, and the holds.

## The words of the user

The decision file gives the six quotes of the user in their order. In short, the hip angle was
"too unnatural". The user first chose a recline toward the chair back, and then asked for a
straight back and a taller chair. Then the user refused a tilted seat, chose the variant L15, and
asked to make the fold between the trunk and the thigh "less obvious".

## Sources

The manager gave these sources for the posture of seated string players:

- violinist.com, discussion 9372: "The main point is that your hips are higher than your knees",
  with blocks under the back legs of the chair.
  <https://www.violinist.com/discussion/archive/9372>
- Internet Cello Society, tim14: chairs "that slope forward instead of backward" and "high enough
  to allow your knees to be below your waist". <https://www.ics.cellobello.org/cnc/tim14.htm>
- The Strad, "Sitting pretty: chairs for musicians": a forward-sloping seat and a seat height that
  adjusts. <https://www.thestrad.com/accessories/sitting-pretty-chairs-for-musicians/10692.article>
- Wenger, musician chair product sheet LT0020: the hips rotated forward and the natural lumbar
  curve. <https://www.wengercorp.com/Lit/Wenger_Musician Chair_PS_LT0020.pdf>

The first agent gave the OSHA eTool "Computer Workstations", reclined sitting: "The user's torso
and neck are straight and recline between 105 and 120 degrees from the thighs". The user then
asked for a straight back, so the round kept the trunk upright and opened the hip angle with the
thighs.

## The way to L15

```text

 hip 88 deg -> (phase 1, dropped:  -> (phase 1b, dropped:   -> L10, L15, L15-long
                "back straight")      "seat should not be      user chose L15
                                       tilted")
```

## The short level seat

`front-scan.sh` moves the front edge of the level seat of the rig back in steps, and prints the
seat top of the rig, the gap of the haunch after `sit()`, the seat of the app, and the edge to the
knees (`front-scan.txt`). The seat of the app normally reaches `seatBack` of `ANIMALS` past the
chair of the rig, so the scan sets `seatBack` to 0 and moves the back edge of the chair of the rig
forward by the old move. The body then keeps its place on the chair.

- Red fox, 15°: the seat of the app is 20.5 cm high up to the front edge 0.09 units, and 21.9 cm
  from 0.08 units back. The thighs leave the seat top between 0.08 and 0.09 units.
- Rabbit, 15°: the seat of the app is 16.1 cm to 16.3 cm high up to the front edge 0, and 17.9 cm
  at -0.01 and -0.02 units. The round haunch of the rabbit rises further back, so the seat top
  rises again behind -0.03 units, with the front edge 18 cm and more behind the knees.

No minimum depth of the chair limits the seat: `chairDepthOf()` of `tools/studio-body.ts` takes the
larger of the depth of the chair of the rig and the gap behind the trunk, and both shrink with the
front edge.

The haunch of the red fox stays 2.0 cm above the seat behind the hip joints (1.6 cm on `main`).
`front-scan.txt` gives the sit rules that the round tried: `ramp` and `end` change nothing, and
`gap` 0 gives 1.64 cm with 6 faces that the bind turns over (3 with the rule of the branch).

## The crease of the belly and the thighs

Cause. The upright trunk over the sloped thighs folds the mesh where the belly meets the top of
each thigh:

- A render of the seated bind of the red fox with the workbench engine of Blender
  (`flat_render.py`, `flat-light.webp`): with a flat light and the face colors, no line shows. With
  a studio light, and with one gray color, the line shows. So the cause is the shade of the
  geometry. The face colors at the seam are 10% darker than the fur of the red fox, and equal to the
  fur of the rabbit (`crease-L15.txt`).
- The close views come from the gallery, which draws no post effect.
- The seam of the red fox: 12 of 22 edges fold more than the crease angle of the smooth normals
  (35°), up to 127°, so the normals split there. 28 pairs of facing trunk and thigh faces lie
  within 2 cm, the nearest 0.43 cm apart: a deep slit. The seam of the rabbit: 6 of 15 edges above
  35°, up to 104°, and no slit.

Rule. `crease_relax()` of `tools/blender/rig_mesh.py` smooths the seated bind around the seam
(`crease` of the rig config). The corrective shapes give the source back, so the standing body
keeps its form.

```text
 seam = vertices of a trunk face and a thigh face, in front of the hip joints
 ring 0 (weight 1) -> ring 1 (2/3) -> ring 2 (1/3) -> outside (0)
 each round: p += share * weight * (mean of the neighbors - p)
```

`dark.py` counts the crease pixels in fixed boxes of the close views, with the same box and the
same thresholds for each column (`crease-pixels.txt`): the dark pixels below a fixed luma, and the
line pixels that differ by more than 18 from the median of the 9 × 9 pixels around them.

## The turn of the trunk and the standing body

The first agent of the round made the turn of the trunk of the rig and found the cause of an error
of the standing body (`standing-probe.ts`, `standing-probe-before.txt`). With a turned trunk,
vertex 744 of the red fox (weights: spine1 0.48, upper arm 0.30, spine 0.12, chest 0.09) missed the
source by 3.5 mm, because the standing body blended it two times: the skin of the standing turns,
then the arm shapes that the rig solved in the seated axes. The export now writes the arm turns of
the clip, and the app solves the arm shapes again for the standing bind. `trunk-shares.txt` gives
the share sets of the turn that the first agent tried at a recline of 20°.
