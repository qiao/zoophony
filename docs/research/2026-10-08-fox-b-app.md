## 2. How the pose code drives the new skeleton

The pose code reads a rest skeleton: no bone has a rotation, the arms hang down, the palms face
the body, and the bow lies in the hanging right hand (design 4.7). The mesh of route B has the
seated playing pose of the sheet. The app keeps the rest skeleton for the pose code, and binds
the mesh at the pose of the sheet:

```text
fox.glb (route B)
   │  pnpm pack-rigged <glb> fox-red
   v
src/models/rigged/fox-red.ts ── joints in the sheet pose, colors, mesh with 4 bones
   │  buildRigged()
   ├──> rest skeleton ──────> poseAt(): IK, holds, idle ──> local rotations ──┐
   │    (arms down,                                                          │
   │     face to the front)                                                  v
   └──> bind frames ────────> scene: inverse bind matrices ──────────────> GPU skin
        (sheet pose)          (instrument bones: rest frames)
```

1. The packer maps the 23 bones onto the bones of the player with the same names. `tail.2` to
   `tail.4` map onto `tail2`. The eyes follow `eyeL` and `eyeR`, so that they blink.
2. The rest skeleton has the joints of the rig for the trunk, the legs, the tail, and the
   shoulders. The arms hang straight down from the shoulder joints with the lengths of the rig.
   The face turns 12° to the violin in the sheet, and the rest skeleton turns it to the front.
3. Each bone gets a bind frame: the smallest turns that move the hanging arm onto the arm joints
   of the mesh, the hand along the hand bone, and the turn of the face.
4. The scene binds each bone at the inverse of its bind frame (`Skeleton.meshBind`). The
   instrument and the bow keep their rest frames, because they have their places in the rest
   skeleton.
5. The body points come from the mesh: the head center, the mouth, the palms, and the finger
   blocks (design 4.4).

The types at the boundary:

```typescript
/** A rigged body: the packed module of a species (models/rigged.ts). */
export interface RiggedBody {
  /** The seat of the model sheet, in meters: 0.35 for the red fox. */
  readonly seatHeight: number;
  /** The color and the finish of each region. */
  readonly regions: readonly SculptRegion[];
  /** Each joint in the pose of the mesh, in the axes of the model. */
  readonly joints: Readonly<Record<RiggedJoint, Vec3>>;
  /** A point of each hand bone 0.1 m from the wrist. */
  readonly handTips: Readonly<Record<'handL' | 'handR', Vec3>>;
  /** The tip of the nose. */
  readonly nose: Vec3;
  /** The packed mesh: positions, four bones and weights for each vertex, triangles. */
  readonly mesh: string;
}

/** The place and the world rotation of one bone (models/rig.ts). */
export interface BoneFrame {
  readonly position: Vec3;
  readonly rotation: Quat;
}

/** The skin of four bones of each corner (models/mesh.ts, MeshData.skin). */
export interface FourBoneSkin {
  /** Four bone indices for each corner. */
  readonly indices: Uint8Array;
  /** Four weights for each corner. They add up to 1. */
  readonly weights: Float32Array;
}
```

A test poses a mesh with four bones and bind frames with `posedCorner()` of the tests and with
the CPU skin of three.js. The two agree within 1e-6 m.

## 6. What remains

- **The bow arm is short.** The rig puts the right elbow 0.13 m from the shoulder, so the arm is
  1.25 H from the shoulder to the wrist (1.56 H on the skeleton of the red fox). The fox bows with
  the half of the hair at the frog. A longer upper arm in the rig gives the full bow.
- **The bow paw on the sheet page covers the muzzle.** The sheet moment plays an open A near the
  frog (design 7.1), so the paw comes up to the chin. The sheet holds the bow paw at the chest. A
  moment in the middle of the bow is nearer to the sheet.
- **A standing violin soloist** of the second violins has the parametric body, because the model
  has no standing legs.
- **The left paw follows the forearm** for most of its vertices, so it does not turn with the
  wrist. A part mask that gives the paw to the hand bone fixes it in `rig.py`.
- **Steps for the next species**, for example the rabbit, which also has a model sheet:
  1. Build the model with the gated pipeline of `tools/spike/fox-gated/` and the rig of the
     species, with the same bone names.
  2. Add the palette roles of its colors to `ROUTE_B_PALETTE` and `speciesRegions()` of
     `tools/pack-rigged.ts`, if they differ from the fox.
  3. Run `pnpm pack-rigged <glb> <species>`, and give the species `rigged` in place of `sculpt`.
  4. Run the tests. The tests of the hold, the clearance, and the sheet page check the new body.
     Check the bow range and the head center against the instrument of the species.
