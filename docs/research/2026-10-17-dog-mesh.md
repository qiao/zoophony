# The dog mesh of the user as the cellist

Date 2026-10-17. Branch `dog-mesh`. Decisions: [`2026-10-17-mesh-dog.md`](../decisions/2026-10-17-mesh-dog.md).
The probes, their outputs, and the images are in [`2026-10-17-dog-mesh/`](2026-10-17-dog-mesh/).
Each command runs from the root of the worktree.

The user gave a textured mesh of a beagle with no skeleton (`assets/models/dog/dog.glb`). The cello
dogs get that mesh by route 3 of `AGENTS.md`: `pnpm rig-mesh dog` rigs it, and
`pnpm studio-body dog` writes the module `src/models/studio/dog.ts`. The work had two phases. The
first phase wrote the rig config with the arm factor 1, a rule of hanging ears, and a mid-report.
The user then chose the size, the arms, the legs, and the place of the cello, and the second phase
made the body of the app, the tools of the role, the cello hold, the rest, the images, and the
docs.

```text
 dog.glb (user) --tools/mesh-rigs/dog.json--> pnpm rig-mesh dog --> dog.rig.glb
                                                                       | pnpm studio-body dog
                                                                       v
 SPECIES.dog.body = 'studio-dog' <-- STUDIO_BODIES <-- src/models/studio/dog.ts
        |
        v
 DOG_HOLD (pose/bowed-rig.ts) --> pnpm hold-fit cellist, pnpm fold-probe cellist,
                                  pnpm pose-depth cellist, pnpm close-views cellist
```

## 1. Phase 1 and the choice of the user

`sections.py`, `arm_axis.py`, `measures.py`, `tail_gap.py`, `ear_parts.py`, and `head_colors.py`
measured the joints, the ears, the tail, and the eyes on cuts of the mesh (`sections-*.txt`,
`arm-axis.txt`, `measures.txt`, `head-colors.txt`). The phase also added the rule of a hanging
ear (`earFlap`, `hanging_ear()` of `tools/blender/rig_mesh.py`): the long ear flaps of the dog
hang beside the head and the neck, and with the flaps on the head, the head height H grew from
0.229 to 0.259 units. `landmark-probe.ts` measured the dog of `main` beside the red fox, the
rabbit, and the cat (`landmark-probe.txt`), and `sizes.txt` gives the sizes of the mid-report.
The image of the mid-report is `mid-report.webp`. The user chose:

- "Fox head height (Recommended)": the standing head top at the head top of the red fox of `main`.
- "Cello reach (Recommended)": the arm factor that reaches the whole fingerboard and the whole bow
  hair, measured again at the new size after the cello hold.
- "1.5x, as the fox (Recommended)": the leg factor 1.5.
- "Between the knees (Recommended)": the knees spread wide (the option said about 30° to 45° out)
  and hold the lower bout of the cello.

The manager added two decisions: the ear twitch goes off for hanging ears, and the fur gets the
stage check of the cat.

## 2. Size, legs, and knees

The standing head top of the red fox of `main`, after its neck of `fox-form`, is 1.570 m
(`landmark-probe.txt`, `node docs/research/2026-10-17-dog-mesh/landmark-probe.ts`). The dog takes
it: 1.433782 m for each unit, with the standing shoulder joints at 1.00078 m (`try-s30.txt`:
`standing: top 1.570 m`). H is 0.328 m, the seat 0.266 m, and the chair 0.259 m deep.

The thighs slope 25° down and turn out by the spread. `try.sh` rigs each spread and
`body-measures.ts` measures it (`try-s8.txt` to `try-s50.txt`):

| Spread | Knee joints apart m | Knees wide outside m | Knee joint z m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8° | 0.304 | 0.405 | -0.209 |
| 25° | 0.410 | 0.498 | -0.193 |
| 30° | 0.439 | 0.523 | -0.185 |
| 35° | 0.467 | 0.547 | -0.177 |
| 45° | 0.517 | 0.591 | -0.156 |
| 50° | 0.539 | 0.611 | -0.144 |

The z of a knee joint is its place in front of the floor point of the seat (the front is -z). The
lower bout of the cello of the app is 0.41 m wide. A wider spread moves the knees back, away from
a cello at the chest. The front of the trunk of the seated dog is at z -0.235 m from the belly to
the chest (`dog-profile.ts`), 4 cm to 5 cm in front of the knee joints of 30°, and about 1 cm in
front of the front of the knees. So the knees cannot reach the lower bout of a cello that rests
against the chest. The spread of 30°, the lower end of the choice, keeps the knees nearest to the
cello, and the knees show on the sides of the lower bout from the front.

## 3. The cello between the knees

Real cellists steady the cello on the lower bout between the knees and on the upper bout against
the upper chest, with the neck above the left shoulder and the C-string peg behind the left ear
(`docs/reference/string-technique.md`, section 2.7, sources 59 to 61). `cello-place.ts` gives the
play shift and the play turn of a place: the endpin tip on the floor, the lean of the axis back
and to the left, and the turn of the top plate. `cello-contact.ts` measures the cello against the
body, the knees, the head, and the ears in the rest and in a play sample.

| Place: endpin x, z m, back, left, turn | Cello in body cm | Knee L, R gap cm | Head gap cm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02, -0.335, 3°, 8°, 5° (rest) | 1.2 | 4.5, 3.5 | 3.0 |
| 0.02, -0.295, 0°, 8°, 0° (rest) | 0.1 | 0.6, 2.7 | 4.2 |
| 0.02, -0.285, 2°, 8°, 5° (rest) | 4.6 | 0.1, 0.0 | 2.4 |
| 0.06, -0.305, 0°, 11°, 0° (final, rest) | 0.3 | 2.0, 2.9 | 6.6 |

A cello that leans back touches the chest only with its lower bout further in front of the knees,
so the final cello stands vertical. The sweep of all demos chose the left lean and the turn of the
top plate (`sweep-place-1.txt`, `sweep-place-2.txt`): with the lean 10° or the turn 5° to the
right, the head came 1.7 cm to 2.0 cm to the neck in the looks at the conductor, and with 12° and
14°, the right upper arm went 2.0 cm and 3.4 cm into the body. With 11° and no turn, the head
keeps at least 4.0 cm. The trunk does not lean: the belly then pushes into the cello.

## 4. Reach and arm factor

`cello-reach.ts` measures the arms against the cello in the playing pose with no idle motion, and
gives the factor that reaches the whole fingerboard and the whole hair
(`node docs/research/2026-10-17-dog-mesh/cello-reach.ts [<hold changes>]`). The probe takes the
end of the hair within 1.1 cm as reached. The factor follows the hold, and each step of the fit
changed it:

| Hold | Left arm factor | Bow arm factor |
|---|---|---|
| The cello of the floor hold, at the size of the mid-report (`cello-b15.txt`) | 1.31 | 1.59 |
| The cello at the chest, the top plate turned 5°, no lean | 1.40 | 1.74 |
| The same, 3 cm to the right, the left paw tilted 60° | 1.47 | 1.80 |
| The shoulder turn 30° | 1.49 | 1.83 |
| The left lean 11°, no turn of the top plate | 1.48 | 1.88 |
| The string planes 12° apart (final) | 1.48 | 1.71 |

With the steps of 5 mm of `bowRangeOf()`, the rig needed 1.91 for the whole hair with the string
planes 17° apart, and needs 1.75 with 12°. The rig takes 1.75: the left paw reaches the fingerboard from 0 to 1, and the bow paw the hair from
0.030 m to 0.525 m of 0.557 m (`cello-reach.txt`). 1.74 reached it to 0.520 m. The reach from the
shoulder joint to the middle of the palm is 0.5709 m, and the red fox has 0.5452 m.

## 5. The fit of the hold

`pnpm hold-fit cellist` fits each variant on `FIT_DEMOS` (the play folds at a step of 1 s, and
the deepest point of each set), and `pnpm pose-depth cellist --gaps` sweeps all demos.

- **The left paw.** With the tilt of the hand of 30°, the thick paw went 3.0 cm into the neck and
  the left forearm 3.3 cm to 3.7 cm into the upper bout (`sweep-place-pole-grid.txt`). With 45°,
  1.7 cm and 2.3 cm, and with 60°, 1.2 cm and 0.3 cm (`sweep-tilt45.txt`, `sweep-tilt60.txt`, one
  demo). The play folds were 70.9% at 40°, 35.1% at 50°, and 25.2% at 60° (`fit-tilt.txt`). The
  tilt became a value of each hold (`BowedHold.tiltL`).
- **The left elbow.** With the pole of the floor hold, the left upper arm went 3.8 cm into the upper
  bout. Out to the left, (-1, -0.6, -0.2), 0.0 cm (`sweep-place-pole-grid.txt`). The poles and the
  turn for each string set the folds: (-1, -0.8, -0.2) with 2° for each string folded 16.5% of the
  play samples, with 5° 21.8%, and with 0° 16.3% (`fit-pole-left-2.txt`, `fit-pole-left-3.txt`).
- **The right elbow.** The raised elbow of the floor hold, (1, 1.4, -0.3), folded the right shoulder
  in 99.9% of the play samples, and the level elbow (1, 0, -0.3) in 34.2% (`fit-pole-right-1.txt`,
  `fit-pole-right-2.txt`). At the tip, (1, 0.3, -1) keeps the right upper arm at the limit of the
  sweep (`sweep-tip-elbow.txt`).
- **The shoulder turn.** The turn of the right shoulder of 45° folded 16.5% of the play samples,
  30° 6.9%, and 25° 9.3% (`fit-shoulder-turn.txt`).
- **The string planes.** At the tip on the C string, the right upper arm went 1.9 cm into the side of
  the chest at the armpit with 17°, 1.5 cm with 13°, and 1.4 cm with 12°, on all demos with the arm
  factor 1.91 (`sweep-string-turn.txt`).

## 6. The folds

`pnpm fold-probe cellist` counts the folds, and `fold-keys.ts` gives the mean turns of the upper arm
and of the forearm in the samples in which named faces fold
(`node docs/research/2026-10-17-dog-mesh/fold-keys.ts <faces> --side R`).

- Rings at the elbows and the wrists, the rings of the cat, folded 56.7% of the play samples
  against 33.3% with no ring, and a ring at the left elbow only 44.4% (`rig-rings.log`). A ring at
  the left elbow or the left wrist made thin faces of the left paw that folded in each sample, and
  the sliver rule for faces up to 0.06 or 0.1 units long folded each sample (`raise-trials.txt`).
- One shape of the raised right arm with the bent elbow of the play (`raise`), at the mean turn
  of the folds of faces 316, 162, 211, and 342 (`keys-right.txt`), with the fade 20° to 35°,
  lowered the play folds from 20.6% (`fold-probe-no-shape.txt`) to 11.4% with the arm factor 1.85.
  The fades 15° to 30° and 10° to 25° folded 11.9% and 13.9%. A second rule of the right arm and
  two rules of the left arm folded more (`raise-trials.txt`). The folds of the left upper arm do
  not gather around one turn: they come with the arm-down shape at 0.375 against 0.099
  (`keys-left.txt`).
- With the final hold and the arm factor 1.75, the cellist folds in 6.7% of the play samples at a
  step of 1 s (`fold-probe-step1.txt`). With the final sit, `pnpm fold-probe cellist` at 0.25 s
  gives 6.3% of the play samples, 12.2% of the lift, and each rest sample, with at most 5 faces of
  the right arm and paw in one rest sample (`fold-probe-cellist.txt`). The faces of the play fold
  in spread poses, each in at most 2.8% of the samples.

## 7. The rest

The cello keeps its place, as a floor instrument now rests at its playing place (`restPivotOf()`),
and the left paw holds its neck at 0.4 of the fingerboard with the elbow out to the left. The right
paw holds the bow upright with the frog over the right knee (`UPRIGHT_BOW`), as a cellist in a
first lesson stands the screw on the right knee with the bow up. The grip 0.4 H out from the top of
the knee keeps each part within 1.2 cm, and with the grip on the knee, the right upper arm went
4.1 cm into the side of the belly (`fit-rest.txt`). `bow-path.ts` measures the bow with a point at
each 1 cm along it in the lift, the rest, and the lowering: no point goes into the body, and the
bow keeps at least 12.8 cm from the music stands (`bow-path.txt`).

## 10. Images

One batch of `pnpm close-views` made the images of the round:

```bash
pnpm close-views batch scratch/images --species dog,cat,fox-red,rabbit \
  --moments play,rest,lift50,lower50 --views front,front35,side,left,back --zoom 1 --zoom 2.5 \
  --role cellist --lineup --camera default --camera violins --base main
```

- `close-views-cellist.webp`: the dog cellist at the frog, at the tip, at a crossing, in the rest,
  and halfway through the lift and the lowering, from the front, both sides, and the back.
  `close-views-cellist-base.webp` puts the parametric dog of `main` above it.
- `species-play-base.webp`, `species-rest-base.webp`, `species-lift50-base.webp`, and
  `species-lower50-base.webp`: the dog, the cat, the red fox, and the rabbit in five views, each
  row of `main` above the row of the branch. `species-play-zoom2.5-base.webp` and
  `species-rest-zoom2.5-base.webp` show the arms and the lap close.
- `lineup-base.webp`: the four species standing side by side, `main` above the branch.
- `play-base.webp`: the play frame of the default demo from the default camera and the camera of
  the violins, `main` above the branch.
