# The bear pea eyes in the face

Date 2026-10-20. Round `bear-eyes`, from `main` 3a6932df. Decisions:
[`2026-10-20-bear-eyes.md`](../decisions/2026-10-20-bear-eyes.md). The probes and their outputs
are in [`2026-10-20-bear-eyes/`](2026-10-20-bear-eyes/). Each command runs from the root of the
tree.

## 1. The question

The user sent a near view of the bear conductor from the front left and above: "also the bear's
eyes are still too far from the face". The flat eye stood out of the face at the top edge of the
muzzle, as a bead on the fur. The goal: the rim at most 0.016 H out of the fur (the worst of the
other studio bodies), a clean oval with its front in the front view, the same size, and a happy
face.

## 2. The cause

`eye-flat.ts` fits a plane to the head surface over the oval at places around the old eye
(`eye-flat-main.log`). At most places within 0.1 H, the surface lies 0.016 H to 0.034 H under
the plane, so no flat oval fits. Only the forehead, 0.04 H in and 0.1 H up, gives 0.009 H to
0.011 H, and it faces 41° to 46° up. The faces of the low-poly head are large: each face near the eye holds a
circle of at most 0.047 H, and the oval is 0.13 × 0.18 H.

A bent oval (`eyeBend()`) fits the surface, but the old place lies on a diagonal edge of the
muzzle. The fit there has a twist term `uv` of 6.5 (axis halfway to the normal) to 9.2 (along the
direction), against at most 2.5 on the other bodies. The principal curvatures of the fit (the
eigenvalues of `[[2 uu, uv], [uv, 2 vv]]` of each bend of `scan-curvature-export.log`, `s3-*`)
are +2.5 to +5.5 and -11 to -14 there, along the diagonals of the oval. A fold along a
diagonal breaks the symmetry of the oval, so it reads as a bean or a teardrop. A fold along an
axis of the oval, as on the dog, keeps a clean outline.

## 3. The routes

`bear-variants.ts` writes the bear module of each variant into `scratch/v/<name>/`, with the
probe settings of `probe-options.patch`. `eye-fit.ts` measures each variant, and `app-eyes.ts`
gives the eye places in the front view. `render.sh` renders each variant from a dev server, and
`grid.py` and `grid-tight.py` make the grids. The names of the variants give the settings: `w` is
`eyeNormal`, `t` the share of the twist term, `o` and `u` the move out and up in H, `s` the turn
toward the front, and `d` a sink in H.

| Route | Variant | Rim out, H | Twist `uv` | Seen at 0°, H² | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `main` | `main`: flat, w 0.5 | 0.027 | 0 | 0.0098 | clean oval, a bead at the muzzle edge |
| 2, sink | `r2-d0.015`: flat, w 0.5 | 0.015 | 0 | 0.0069 | 54% of the eye shows |
| 3, twist cut | `s2-w0.5-t0-o0-u0` | 0.019 | 0 | 0.0083 | the muzzle cuts the lower part |
| 3, twist cut | `s2-w0.5-t0.5-o0-u0` | 0.016 | 3.25 | 0.0084 | teardrop from above |

| 4, front axis | `f1`: bent, s 1 | 0.011 | 17.2 | 0.0141 | stretched over the muzzle |

The numbers are in `routes.log`, `scan-twist.log` (`s2-*`), `scan-axis.log` (`f*`, with its
settings in its first lines), `scan-front.log` (`s4-*`), and `scan-places.log` (`s5-*`). The images are `variants-twist.webp` and `variants-twist-near.webp`
(the twist cut), `variants-front.webp` (the front axis), and `variants-places.webp` (the moves).
Each grid shows `main` in the first column, and the rows front, 60°, and side at 10° elevation,
then front and 60° at 45°.

Below the old place, the eye place crosses an edge of the head between u -0.02 and u -0.03. Below
it, the face is a shallow dish with a twist of 0.9 to 2.7, but it faces the side, and the front
view shows only 0.0063 H² to 0.0083 H² of the eye. Just above the edge, at o 0.1 and u -0.02,
the twist is 3.36, and 0.0086 H² shows.

## 6. The result

The bear eye place moves out 0.1 H and down 0.02 H before it goes to the head surface
(`eyeShift` of `ANIMALS`), and the oval bends as on the other studio bodies. The flat eyes
(`flatEyes`) have no species now, so the setting goes. The rim lies 0.011 H out, within the
0.009 H to 0.016 H of the other bodies, and the places are nearer to the sheet.

Open: the front view shows 12% less eye area than `main`, and the twist of 3.36 is above the 2.5
of the other bodies. From the front and above, the lower end of each oval is a little pointed
(`pairs-above.webp`).
